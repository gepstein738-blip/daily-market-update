## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### September NFP — The Number That Moved Everything

**+29,000 jobs** against a consensus of **+84,000**. This isn't a miss — it's a warning shot. The unemployment rate ticked to **4.2%** from 4.1%. Revisions made it worse: August revised down to +133,000 from prior +162,000; July revised to -10,000. Mohamed El-Erian called it "weak across the board" and said labor demand is "flashing yellow." Private payrolls came in at +46,000 vs. +85,000 expected.

The market's immediate reaction was textbook: yields fell, stocks rallied, dollar softened, gold bid. Prediction markets moved to **85% probability of a hold** at the October 28 FOMC. This is the first genuinely soft labor market print in this cycle that's come without an obvious one-time distortion. Watch whether October's print confirms a trend or this is weather/revision noise.

### Fed

**VP Jefferson** and **Governor Bowman** both signaled desire to hold steady — notable because Bowman has been the most hawkish regional voice for the past year. **NY Fed President Williams** said "no need for urgency," though left the door open for "one further upward adjustment" late in the year (i.e., December). **Minneapolis Fed President Kashkari** more equivocal — said he doesn't know how high rates need to go, which is the honest answer. Two remaining meetings: October 28 and December 9.

The subtext here: the Fed wants to hold, the data just gave them cover. The real risk is October jobs (released November 6) coming in strong and reopening the December hike debate. Don't book a pivot too aggressively.

### Geopolitics

The US deploying a **third carrier strike group** to the Middle East is the single biggest macro wildcard of the next 30 days. Markets aren't fully pricing the tail risk on Strait of Hormuz disruption. Iran produces ~3.3M bpd; Hormuz carries roughly 20% of global oil and a significant share of LNG. The IEA has already flagged a 1.78M bpd supply deficit scenario if Hormuz disruptions deepen. China halted fuel exports this week — an underappreciated move that tightens Asian energy balances further.

France fiscal dynamics are quietly becoming systemic. The French-German spread hitting euro-crisis wides is not priced into EUR/USD, European bank equity, or US IG spread sheets. If you're not tracking this, start.

### Central Banks (ex-Fed)

UK: 30-year gilt at 6% — the BOE is in a difficult position with inflation still sticky and the long end blowing out. No meeting today but expect emergency language by next week if gilts don't settle. Japan: BOJ holding steady; USD/JPY at 157.77 still elevated, but yen firmed post-NFP as yield differentials compress. No policy change expected until December at earliest.

## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### The Week's Story: The CPU Is Back

This week will be remembered as the moment Wall Street woke up to the **CPU renaissance thesis**. The proximate catalyst was **Meta's Muse AI agent**, which demonstrated that persistent agentic AI workloads are overwhelmingly CPU-bound rather than GPU-bound at the orchestration/operating layer. The numbers being thrown around: CPU-to-GPU ratios of 4:1 to 40:1 for agentic workloads, versus the previous paradigm of 1:8 favoring GPUs. This isn't a NVDA bearish story — NVDA hit a record high today. This is an additive story that lifts INTC and ARM.

**Intel (INTC) — the week's biggest surprise**

Up 11% today and **+17.3% on the week**. Closed at $123.82 intraday. Intel had been left for dead relative to AMD and NVDA for four years. The Muse catalyst reopened the CPU debate and Intel is sitting at the crossroads of three tailwinds: agentic AI CPU demand, a semiconductor sector melt-up, and a favorable macro backdrop (falling yields reducing discount rate on unprofitable-to-turning-profitable stories). Intel has also been quietly executing on its Hot Chips 2026 architecture announcements for agentic AI. This is not a short-squeeze — it's a fundamental re-rating. The question is whether Muse sustains as a deployment pattern or gets replaced by GPU-optimized inference in 12 months.

**Nvidia (NVDA) — all-time high, $237+**

Morgan Stanley reinstated as **Top Semiconductor Pick** with an Overweight and a $300 price target. Post-meetings with CEO Jensen Huang and CFO Colette Kress, the analyst highlighted that developments around land, power, data center shells, financing, and component constraints "all play to their strengths." NVDA hit a fresh all-time high, first new record since May, erasing concerns about valuation stretch. Apple briefly eclipsed NVDA's market cap ($4.95T vs. $4.77T) but that battle is fluid. The MS reinstatement carries weight — the firm had been lukewarm through the summer selloff.

**ARM Holdings (ARM) — +6.94% today, +177% YTD**

Closed October 1 at $292.34; surging again today. ARM is the infrastructure layer that benefits from both GPU AND CPU expansion — its architecture underpins next-gen data center CPUs and agentic AI execution. The 177% YTD gain means the stock has front-run fundamentals, and consensus PT of ~$303 implies minimal further upside from here on pure price target math. The longer-duration bull case ($612 targets being floated) requires assuming ARM captures the full addressable market of CPU compute in AGI data centers. Trim the edges or define your time horizon carefully.

**Micron (MU) — the context you need**

MU's FY2026 (ended August 2026) was one for the record books: revenue of **$133.2B, up 256% YoY**; Q4 alone at $54.2B vs $50.45B expected; EPS $33.42 vs $31.16; gross margin expanded to 81.1% for the full year. Stock is up **270% YTD**. This drove a sector halo effect that lifted the entire memory/storage space. The Micron numbers changed the narrative around HBM (high-bandwidth memory) supply and pricing — demand for AI training/inference is consuming HBM capacity as fast as it can be built.

**Seagate (STX) — -10.3%, the counterpoint**

STX hit pre-market $848 vs. prior close $945.57 on Toshiba announcing a major storage capacity expansion. The storage market for cold AI data (model weights, checkpoints, training archives) has been a high-margin tailwind for STX for 18 months. Toshiba entering aggressively threatens pricing power. At $900+ the market was pricing Seagate as a near-monopoly beneficiary. Toshiba's move breaks that thesis.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the tape is telling you

**The dominant theme this week is the collision of two powerful forces: a geopolitical oil spike and a domestic labor market that is genuinely softening.** The oil spike won on Thursday (yields up, stocks down); the jobs print won on Friday (yields down, stocks up). Net-net the week is roughly flat for equities but the bond market has moved dramatically — 10Y touched 5.35%+ intraday Thursday and is now closing near 5.29%, having been at 4.75% six weeks ago.

**The CPU renaissance is a real trade, not a story.** INTC +17% and ARM +12% in a single week isn't noise. Meta's Muse deployment architecture is a legitimate data point. The debate worth having: is this a structural shift in AI compute demand or a trading catalyst that reverses once the narrative is fully priced? Given Intel's current capacity constraints and the lead times on server CPU procurement, even a 10-15% shift in the CPU-to-GPU ratio represents a multi-year demand tailwind for Intel and ARM. This trade is early, not late.

**ACN +15.8% is the most important data point for AI monetization in 2026.** Every bear on tech AI monetization needs to explain Accenture's consulting demand. Enterprise AI deployment is accelerating. The picks-and-shovels (consulting, cloud, data infrastructure) are monetizing before the application layer.

### What the market is not pricing

1. **UK fiscal / gilt crisis contagion.** 30-year gilts at 6%, French-German spread at euro-crisis wides — US credit markets are behaving as if these are European-only problems. They are not. UK pension fund leverage and European bank exposure to French sovereign debt have global transmission mechanisms. US IG credit spreads are too tight if these dynamics deteriorate further.

2. **The October jobs revision risk.** This NFP print (+29k) ended with downward revisions to July (-10k) and August (-29k revision). Three consecutive downward revisions suggests BLS data quality issues or a genuine labor market deterioration that's been masked. If November 6's October jobs number also misses, you're looking at a genuine recessionary signal, not just a soft landing.

3. **Strait of Hormuz tail risk.** The market is pricing $99 Brent, not $150. Three carrier strike groups in the region means the US has already made a deterrence statement. The question is whether deterrence holds. A single tanker incident in the Strait sends oil through $120 within 48 hours. You are not being paid for this optionality in oil right now.

4. **NVDA at $237 with a $300 PT means 27% upside — but the stock is at an all-time high.** Morgan Stanley's reinstatement is bullish catalysis, not a floor. The Mag 7 concentration risk in passive funds means any de-risking event will be amplified. Understand your portfolio's NVDA/Mag7 beta before celebrating today's record.

### Risks to the consensus

- **Consensus**: Soft landing, Fed holds, AI capex sustains, equities grind higher into year-end.
- **Risk 1**: Oil spike on Hormuz event disrupts soft-landing narrative; stagflationary shock re-prices every asset class simultaneously.
- **Risk 2**: UK pension/gilt crisis worsens; BoE forced to emergency QE again; EUR falls hard; EUR/USD tests 1.05; USD spike crushes EM.
- **Risk 3**: Agentic AI CPU demand story proves durable — Intel significantly undervalued; the market hasn't fully rerated yet.
- **Risk 4**: November jobs report comes in similarly weak; recession risk pricing re-enters equities.
