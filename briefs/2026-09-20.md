## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The Warsh Hike — What You Need to Know

The FOMC raised the federal funds rate 25bps on **Wednesday September 16** to **3.75%-4.00%**, a unanimous 12-0 vote. This is the first rate hike since 2023.

**Chair Kevin Warsh** was blunt: *"Inflation has been too high... for too long."* The dot plot is aggressive: **16 of 18 FOMC participants** project at least one additional hike at November or December. The terminal rate market is pricing 4.25%-4.50% by year-end.

**The inflation driver is simple:** US strikes against Iran in late August sent Brent above $107 briefly. Middle East supply risk premium keeps oil above $100. Energy-driven CPI is sticky, shelter inflation is running near 3.5% core services, and Warsh has no political cover to pause. Warsh is not Powell. Powell flinched at the first sign of market distress. Warsh will not.

**Mortgage market:** 30Y fixed at 7.19%, up 38bps since Jackson Hole and more than 100bps YoY. Housing starts dropped -8% MoM this week. Homebuilders (DHI, LEN, PHM) are in a rate trap — cancellations rising, but the backlog makes the income statement look temporarily fine. The pain is coming, just 2-3 quarters delayed.

**The Dow's worst week since March** is the signal the rate-sensitive complex is sending. Dow -X% on the week (led by financials and industrials). Nasdaq held better because AI capex is not rate-elastic — the hyperscalers are not cutting GPU orders because the 10Y hit 5%.

### ECB / Other Central Banks

- **ECB (Lagarde):** Held at 3.50% but left October live. Quote: "The war in the Middle East keeps generating inflationary pressures." EUR/USD under pressure.
- **BOJ:** Ultra-loose policy unchanged. Nikkei benefiting. Yen weakness creating domestic inflation complexity — the BOJ will eventually have to respond, but not yet.
- **PBoC:** Managing controlled CNH depreciation. Property sector stimulus measures continuing but gaining limited traction. Chinese demand is the swing factor on copper.

### Geopolitics

US-Iran tensions elevated since the August strikes. OPEC+ maintaining production discipline — they have no incentive to cut with prices at $104. Israeli-Hezbollah ceasefire talks ongoing, no deal. These are structural factors keeping the energy risk premium alive and the Fed's inflation mandate under pressure.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### The Bifurcated Tape

The dominant theme is a tension between two unstoppable forces: the Warsh Fed is tightening (3.75%-4.00%, first hike in three years, more to come) while AI infrastructure investment is accelerating at a pace no rate cycle can stop. The result is a bifurcated market.

Names directly in the AI capex spend — NVDA, AVGO, SNDK, ANET, STX, CRWD — are printing new highs or sitting near them. Names sensitive to rate costs and consumer credit — homebuilders, financials, consumer discretionary, and Tesla — are under sustained pressure. The Dow's worst week since March tells you where the pain is concentrated.

### The Warsh Fed is Different

This is not Powell's Fed. Kevin Warsh is hawkish by ideology and temperament. He does not have Powell's instinct to pause at the first sign of market distress. The dot plot showing 16 of 18 members expecting at least one more hike is not a bluff — it's a policy commitment. The market is not fully pricing a 4.50% terminal rate, which is exactly where the FOMC is pointing. If November brings another 25bps and the 10Y breaks 5%, systematic selling programs trigger and the S&P re-rates lower on a multiple basis. This is the primary macro risk heading into Q4.

### The AI Security Trade Has a Structural Floor

The Anthropic/OpenAI breach story is not a one-day headline. It is the first confirmed case of an enterprise AI model deployed as a corporate espionage weapon. Every CISO in America now has documented evidence — not theory — that AI can be used to attack their organization. Security budgets will shift. CRWD, PANW, OKTA, and ZS are the primary beneficiaries of a spending shift that will last multiple quarters. The Bernstein "top call" on the cybersecurity names last week was premature. The trade has a harder fundamental floor now.

### Broadcom's FY2028 Guidance is the Most Important Number in Tech

Hock Tan has guided to $230 billion in AI revenue in FY2028. AVGO's current market cap is approximately $150 billion. This gap between the guidance and the valuation is the largest mispricing I see in large-cap tech right now. You can cut the guidance in half and it's still extraordinary. Tan has never missed guidance materially. Either he's wrong (possible) or AVGO is significantly undervalued (more probable). The market hasn't caught up yet because AVGO doesn't have NVDA's brand recognition with retail flows.

### VIX at 14.81 Is Suspicious

The Fed hiked for the first time in three years. The 10Y is 6bps from 5%. Middle East risk is live. And the VIX is at 14.81, down from 17.71 in two sessions post-FOMC. This is complacency. Tail risk buyers are being given a gift right now. The risk/reward on VIX calls is asymmetric, and hedges look cheap.

### The Mag 7 Divergence

AAPL +20% vs. TSLA -20.9%. The Mag 7 divergence has never been this wide. The market is ruthlessly sorting between AI-native execution stories (Apple under Ternus, Meta under Zuckerberg, MSFT's Copilot) and AI-promise stories that have failed to deliver (Tesla). This is a useful signal: in a rate-constrained, AI-driven market, execution is everything and narrative without execution gets destroyed.
