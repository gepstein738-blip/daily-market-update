## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve — The Big One

The FOMC delivered a **25bp hike on September 16**, moving the fed funds target range to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first rate increase since 2023 and, critically, the resumption of a tightening cycle that markets had assumed was finished. Vote was **12-0**.

The language was explicit: "Inflation remains elevated." The updated dot plot is the more revealing document: **16 of 18 FOMC participants** see at least one additional hike as likely before year-end, with **4 of 18 seeing two more**. The next FOMC is late October — right before Election Day. This is now a political story as well as a macro one (more on that below).

Powell's press conference took a measured tone — no panic, no urgency, but no room for dovish re-interpretation either. The message: they came back to hike because the data told them to. Services inflation and sticky shelter costs remain the problem.

**Treasury market reaction**: 10Y barely moved (4.951%, flat). The front end priced in the hike and one more. This is not a Volcker moment — this is a scalpel, not a sledgehammer. The question is whether the October hike happens or whether cooling crude and a softening housing print (Lennar data) gives the Fed a reason to pause.

**Watch**: Chicago Fed President and several board members are on the circuit today. Any softening in language could move rates and re-price the dollar quickly.

### Bank of Japan

BoJ raised its benchmark rate to **1.25%**, a 31-year high. The vote was a split — not unanimous — which is the tell. The BoJ is normalizing, but the governing board is not of one mind about pace. USD/JPY has settled near 143; the consensus has been expecting further JPY appreciation, but the split vote caps it. Real money long yen is still the crowded trade and the pair is range-bound until the next BoJ meeting in late October.

### US Tariffs and Trade

US–China trade continues to restructure. US AI-related imports nearly doubled to **$260 billion** — this is a structural trend, not a blip. ASEAN is the clear winner in supply chain diversification: ASEAN imports from China +24% to $161B while ASEAN exports to the US climbed +18% to $108B. The EU is the loser: squeezed on both sides. Section 338 tariffs were updated in the Tax Foundation's September tracker. Net-net: tariffs have re-routed trade but haven't killed aggregate volumes — just the bilateral US-China flows, which are down ~30%.

### Federal Elections Context

The Fed's rate hike timing — just weeks before midterm elections — is politically charged. Multiple news outlets flagged the CNN piece: "How the Fed's rate decisions could hurt GOP chances in the midterms." The White House has not publicly commented. Markets are not pricing electoral outcomes but the optics of hiking into a vote create optionality on political pressure should the October meeting approach with softening data.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape is Telling You

The S&P 500 at 7,729 on a triple witching Friday following the Fed's first hike in three years is a remarkable outcome — and that is precisely why you should be skeptical. The market is behaving as if the Fed hike is the all-clear for AI-driven growth to continue unimpeded. The logic: if the Fed is only hiking 25bp, inflation is manageable; if inflation is manageable, real rates won't blow out; if real rates don't blow out, long-duration AI growth equities can maintain their multiples.

That logic works until it doesn't. Here is what could break it:

**1. The October Hike**: 16 of 18 FOMC members see another hike. If October CPI doesn't cooperate (and oil's current slide helps but shelter inflation is stickier), you get another 25bp in late October and an increasingly stressed consumer. The housing data from Lennar is a preview.

**2. The AI Revenue Cliff**: AVGO at +221% AI semi revenue YoY and MU at +346% YoY are extraordinary numbers. But hyperscaler capex eventually plateaus. The question for Q4 and 2027 guidance is not whether these companies are growing — it's whether the rate of change is sustainable or whether we're at peak rate-of-change. Watch for any hyperscaler capex guidance revision. A single Microsoft or Amazon CAPEX reduction statement could reset semi expectations across the board.

**3. AMZN as the Canary**: Amazon's relative underperformance (+4% YTD vs. 19-31% for Mag 7 peers) is worth watching. AWS growth is solid but not blowout. If AWS doesn't inflect higher on AI inference demand in Q3 results, the market will need to rethink the "all hyperscalers benefit equally" narrative.

**4. Political Tail Risk**: The Fed hiking 6 weeks before midterms is genuinely unusual. If the political blowback results in any pressure on the Fed's independence narrative, real rates could re-price sharply. Not a base case, but worth owning optionality.

**5. The AI Safety Wild Card**: The Anthropic/OpenAI security incident is a preview of regulatory acceleration. Mandatory incident reporting, potential model access restrictions, and EU AI Act enforcement timelines are all compressing. AI companies with pure enterprise exposure are more protected; consumer-facing AI plays are more vulnerable.

**Dominant Flows This Week**: Semiconductor and AI infrastructure continues to absorb capital. Energy sector is getting hit on crude slide. Homebuilders are rolling over. Small-cap IWM is barely positive — this is still a Magnificent-plus-semis rally, not a broad market advance.

**What the Market is NOT Pricing**:
- The probability that this tightening cycle produces a credit event in commercial real estate (office vacancy rates, regional bank exposure) before year-end
- Political noise around the Fed independence question as October approaches
- A potential re-rating of AI hardware multiples if any major hyperscaler cuts capex guidance
