## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The Only Thing That Matters This Week: September CPI (October 14)

Everything in macro this week is secondary to Tuesday's September CPI print. The Cleveland Fed nowcast has September CPI at +3.63% YoY (core +2.30%). Admiral Markets consensus is 3.6–3.7% YoY. August CPI held at 3.4% — so the September read, driven heavily by gasoline's 27%+ YoY contribution, represents a genuine reacceleration.

The FOMC meets October 28. If September CPI comes in at or above 3.6%, the probability of a 25bps hike at October 28 moves meaningfully above the current 16–18% market pricing. If it's clean at 3.4% or below — unlikely on current nowcasts — the Fed is on hold and Nasdaq rips. **This is the binary that defines the week.**

### Fed / Kevin Warsh
Warsh hiked 25bps to 3.75–4.00% on September 16 in a unanimous vote — his first FOMC decision since being appointed by Trump. The market expected a hold; Warsh delivered a hike while citing: (1) stronger-than-expected economy, (2) inflation that had not slowed sufficiently, and (3) heightened geopolitical tensions as uncertainty factors, not reasons to wait.

This puts Warsh directly at odds with Trump, who appointed him expecting an accommodative Fed. Watch for any White House pressure communications this week. Warsh's press conference messaging was notably hawkish: "removing accommodation" language, not "maintaining restrictiveness" — a subtle signal he sees further moves as possible.

16 of 19 FOMC members penciled in at least one more hike in 2026 on the September dot plot. By December, hike odds stand at 75–85%.

### Geopolitics
- **Russia-Ukraine energy ceasefire:** Announced Friday, credited to Trump intermediaries. Oil dropped from $107 to $102. Ceasefire appears to be unraveling as of Sunday night; Brent back to $105+. Treat as noise unless a durable deal is formalized.
- **Iran/Houthis:** Warsh cited geopolitical tensions in September; Red Sea Houthi attacks continuing to add a risk premium to Brent.
- **Japan currency:** USD/JPY at 158.6 is effectively at the MOF's pain threshold. No intervention announced, but the language has escalated.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

The S&P ended last week at or near a record, up 1.2% for the week — and hedge funds just reported their fastest increase in Mag 7 exposure since 2023. That is a crowded, momentum-driven positioning that is not yet resolved. The July 23 Mag 7 selloff ($797B in one session) happened from a similar crowded setup, triggered by earnings. October earnings season is the obvious candidate for the next positioning flush.

The dominant flows this week are defensive positioning ahead of Tuesday's CPI. The oil headline risk is additive to inflation expectations. If CPI comes in hot (≥3.6%), the narrative instantly becomes: Warsh hikes again October 28, 10-year breaks 5.50%, multiple compression accelerates, and every rate-sensitive sector (tower REITs, long-duration tech, utilities) gives back today's gains violently. CCI/AMT up 15% today is the setup — they're the most exposed to a CPI surprise.

### The Consensus View (And Why It May Be Wrong)
Consensus: "AI capex cycle is intact, Mag 7 earnings will beat, October FOMC is a hold, 2027 rate cuts are coming." The tape is generally trading that view.

**What the market is not pricing:**
1. **The AI revenue problem.** SOXX down 5% last week was the first real warning shot. $650B+ in combined hyperscaler capex in 2026 is massive; the question of where the revenue comes from is accelerating. OpenAI's revenue has grown dramatically but is still a fraction of what the infrastructure spend implies in customer-level ROI. If ASML or TSMC guides cautiously this week, the consensus AI capex narrative is genuinely challenged.

2. **The Warsh-Trump tension.** Warsh hiked in September against Trump's wishes. Trump appointed Warsh expecting accommodation. This is not a stable equilibrium. Presidential pressure on the Fed chair is a tail risk to institutional credibility that markets are completely ignoring — 0% probability of Fed independence break is likely wrong.

3. **Japan.** USD/JPY at 158+ is one BOJ decision away from a global carry trade unwind. The yen carry trade is enormous. When it unwinds — as it did briefly in August 2024 — it hits everything simultaneously. The market is not pricing a yen shock.

4. **$5.24% 10-year is NOT priced into forward P/E.** The S&P at 7,865 implies a forward P/E somewhere in the 20–22x range. That is historically extreme at 5.24% risk-free rates. The reconciliation is either rates come down (consensus) or multiples compress (which would be painful). A CPI surprise accelerates that reconciliation.

### Sector Rotation Signal
Tower REITs (CCI, AMT) +15% today tells you the market *believes* rates are coming down eventually. Those moves are long-duration, rate-sensitive instruments pricing in a more dovish second half of 2027. It's a bet on the Fed cutting even after another potential hike. If CPI is hot Tuesday, those positions get crushed. Worth watching as a real-time sentiment indicator for rate expectations.
