## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The PCE Print — Read It Carefully
August core PCE at 3.0% YoY is the cleanest inflation print of the year. The deceleration is real: the monthly run-rate of 0.2% annualizes to ~2.4%, which is in the zip code of the Fed's 2% target. But the year-ago comparison will get harder in Q4, and energy remains a wildcard tied to the Iran conflict. The market is sniffing a Fed pause — the probability of the priced-in November hike has come off ~15 points since the print. Chair Warsh's post-September-16 messaging was unambiguous: "inflation remains too high." But one meaningfully soft print changes the calculus. Watch for Fed speakers today to thread the needle between acknowledging the data and not declaring victory.

### Federal Reserve
The FOMC hiked 25 bps to **3.75%-4.00% on September 16** in a unanimous 12-0 vote. This is the first hike cycle since 2023, restarted in response to the Iran-war-driven energy price surge that pushed headline CPI back above 4.5% and core above 3.5% earlier this year. Markets are pricing approximately **one more 25-bps hike** — the question is whether today's soft PCE starts to soften that call. The Fed's dot plot year-end projection range is 4.1%-4.4%. Fed funds are currently at 3.875% (midpoint).

### Iran War — The Macro Overhang Nobody Is Trading Right
The US-Iran conflict, now approximately three months old, has created a persistent energy supply disruption centered on the Strait of Hormuz through which 20%+ of global oil trade transits. The direct knock-on: Brent crude ran from ~$78 pre-conflict to $109 peak in early September before pulling back to $97 on truce-adjacent signals. The secondary effect is what's driving the Fed's hiking cycle restart — energy pass-through into services inflation has been the Fed's worst nightmare, particularly in transportation (transportation services CPI +1.4% in August alone). Saudi Arabia intercepted Houthi missiles as recently as September 25; the truce remains fragile. Any re-escalation takes Brent back to $105+ and changes the Fed calculus entirely.

### Geopolitical Context
The Iran war's economic impact has been well-documented at this point — a Dallas Fed working paper quantified it as adding ~1.2 percentage points to US CPI over six months. What's less priced is the asymmetric tail: a Strait of Hormuz full closure scenario has $140 oil in the base case. The market is not pricing that. The fact that Brent is at $97 — which is above pre-war levels but well off the September highs — suggests the tape is treating this as a manageable disruption. That complacency has a price.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You
Today is a quarter-end relief rally sitting on top of a very messy underlying story. The PCE print gave risk assets permission to bounce — yields down modestly, dollar soft, equities futures up, gold bid. But don't mistake a tactical bounce for a structural turn. Let's be precise about what the tape is and isn't saying:

**What the tape is saying:**
1. The AI infrastructure trade is alive. NVDA at $108B quarterly guidance, TSMC +46% YTD, AMD making an $8.2B AI bet — the hyperscaler capex cycle is not slowing. OpenAI at $1.4T valuation is still attracting $30B at a time. The compute buildout is the dominant secular trade.
2. Inflation is decelerating but the Fed is not declaring victory. Core PCE at 3.0% is progress; it's not 2.0%. Warsh's Fed has credibility on the line after restarting hikes.
3. FICO's collapse is a regulatory inflection point. When the FHFA moves to commoditize FICO's near-monopoly scoring system, it is a signal that pricing power in data/analytics oligopolies is not permanent. This has read-through to other data businesses.
4. Consumer spending is resilient but selective. Conagra beat estimates; retail credit data is uneven. The Iran-war-driven energy tax on consumers is showing up in discretionary spending pressure.

**What the market is NOT pricing that it should be:**
1. **Iran war re-escalation tail**: Brent at $97 implies a manageable disruption. A Strait of Hormuz closure scenario has $140 oil and another leg of the Fed hiking cycle. The market is treating geopolitical risk as a known known with a defined ceiling — it's not.
2. **Credit curve uninversion risk**: The 10Y-2Y spread turning positive is historically the point where corporate credit stress accelerates, not the all-clear signal. Every credit crunch in history followed — not preceded — the uninversion. HY spreads are complacent.
3. **Mag 7 capex cycle reversal**: The OpenAI, NVDA, and hyperscaler numbers are all pointing to explosive AI capex. What happens when one of the Mag 7 blinks and cuts AI spending guidance? The entire AI infrastructure trade (semis, data centers, power infrastructure) reprices.
4. **FICO/data oligopoly regulatory risk breadth**: The FHFA ruling on FICO is not a one-off. The current administration has been signaling willingness to use regulatory tools to break data pricing power. Look at credit bureaus (EFX, EXP), financial data (MSCI, ICE data, FDS), and specialty analytics firms. This is the start of a theme, not the end.

### Dominant Weekly Themes
- **AI arms race entering a new cost curve**: OpenAI at $1.4T with $40B ARR growing at 70%. The ROI math on AI investment becomes a serious conversation when the models are being monetized at this rate.
- **Fed pivot window**: One soft PCE does not a pivot make, but the conversation has started. Watch for Warsh's language closely in the next Fed communication.
- **Energy geopolitics**: Iran war is the macro overhang. Oil at $97 is the current equilibrium; tail risk is wider than the tape implies.
- **Platform expansion in retail fintech**: HOOD's product blitz is not isolated — the retail trading platform wars are escalating. Watch WeBull, Public, and even Coinbase as competitive responses come.
