## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Fed and Rates

The FOMC meeting is **October 27–28** — next week. Current policy rate: **3.75%–4.00%** (unchanged since September 16 meeting). Markets are pricing approximately 78% odds of a hold at the October meeting. The more relevant question is December.

With CPI printing **3.7% YoY** in September (up from 3.4% in August) and PPI running at **+6.3% YoY** (September wholesale prices), the inflation trajectory is re-accelerating. The US budget deficit swelled to **~$2 trillion** for FY2026 — a structural fiscal impulse that is keeping aggregate demand sticky. Add oil at $100+ and you have a macro setup that is genuinely difficult for the Fed. Markets are now pricing nearly **90% probability of a December rate hike.** If oil holds here into year-end, that probability goes to 95+%.

**What the market is sleeping on:** The Fed has been on a cutting cycle since mid-2025. A re-hike cycle — even a single 25bp move — is a narrative shift that would pressure multiples far more than the absolute rate level suggests. The repricing risk is non-linear. Watch the 10-year. If it breaks above 5.36% (this month's recent high), the tape gets very difficult very quickly.

### Central Banks Globally

No major decisions today. ECB maintained rates at its October 15 meeting. BOJ remains on hold with USD/JPY near 158.50 — any yen strength catalyst (BOJ hawkish surprise) would create dislocations in carry trades that are funding positions in US equities.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

**What the tape is telling you:**

The tape is screaming *rate sensitivity* from every direction. Oil above $100, CPI re-accelerating to 3.7%, PPI at 6.3%, and a $2T budget deficit are conspiring to push the 10-year back toward 5.15% and put a December hike firmly on the table. The market had priced a benign disinflation scenario through mid-2026. That scenario is cracking.

The bifurcation in pre-market tells the story cleanly: Dow +0.22%, Nasdaq -0.50%. Financials (banks beating on NII), energy (HAL, oil stocks), and value industrials are absorbing capital rotating out of high-multiple growth. This is not panic — it's a slow, deliberate reallocation.

**Dominant themes driving flows this week:**

1. **AI capex sustainability.** The fundamental question of whether $800B in annual data center spending generates adequate ROI is unresolved. Every AI-adjacent stock is discounting a best-case scenario; the tape is stress-testing that. CRWV bonds at 15% yield vs. CRWV stock at $60 is a fascinating divergence — credit says "this is risky," equity says "this is a growth story." One of them is wrong.

2. **Re-inflation / re-hike risk.** Three months ago the market was pricing terminal rate cuts through 2026. Now it's pricing a December hike at 90% probability. That's a 180-degree narrative shift. Every duration-sensitive asset — growth tech, REITs, utilities — is repricing. We are early in this repricing.

3. **Big Tech earnings as narrative reset.** Netflix tonight is the appetizer; Tesla/Alphabet/Microsoft/Meta/Amazon/Apple over the next two weeks are the main course. If the prints don't justify the AI capex story (i.e., if Azure/AWS/GCP growth disappoints, if ad revenue softens, if hardware demand slows), the multiple compression accelerates. If they beat broadly, the sell-off last week was a buying opportunity.

4. **M&A signaling confidence in specific verticals.** The Tradeweb/Trumid deal tells you that electronic bond trading is a must-own capability. The Merck KGaA/Silence deal tells you RNA therapeutics is a legitimate M&A currency. Healthcare and financial infrastructure M&A is healthy; consumer and industrial M&A is quiet.

**Risks to the current consensus:**

- **Consensus is that rates will peak in December and no more hikes follow.** The risk is that a 3.7% CPI print is not the peak — that Q4 CPI accelerates toward 4% as energy comparisons flip from favorable to unfavorable. A 4% CPI print changes everything.
- **Consensus is that AI capex is permanently elevated.** The risk is hyperscaler CFO discipline — one major capex guidance cut at any of the Mag 7 earnings calls and the entire AI infrastructure complex reprices violently.
- **Consensus is that geopolitical risk is priced in.** The risk is that it isn't — especially in the Middle East, where an escalation that takes Brent to $120 would be a simultaneous shock to consumers, to the Fed's rate path, and to corporate margin assumptions.

**What the market is not pricing that it should be:**

The market is not adequately pricing the **labor market tightness** that is showing up in selective sectors. The Micron labor action is one data point. Wage inflation for skilled tech and engineering labor remains elevated. If AI productivity gains don't materialize at the speed priced by the market, the combination of elevated wages + high rates + high energy costs compresses corporate margins in a way that 2026 consensus earnings estimates have not fully accounted for.

Also not priced: the structural fiscal position. A $2T deficit at 3.75–4.00% policy rates means the Treasury is issuing an enormous amount of debt into a market where the Fed is no longer the buyer of last resort. This supply overhang is a persistent upward pressure on yields that most equity models are not capturing correctly.
