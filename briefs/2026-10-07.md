## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### The Centerpiece Today: Constellation Energy + Google Nuclear PPA

**CEG** is up 8.86%+ to $291.34 on a landmark 20-year Power Purchase Agreement with **Alphabet/Google** for 890 MW of nuclear capacity across 11 Constellation-owned plants in Illinois, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey. Google also committed to using Constellation's Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise to build an "AI for energy" operational blueprint.

This is bigger than a utility deal. It confirms what has been whispered for months: **hyperscalers are turning to nuclear as the only scalable, baseload, carbon-free power source that can actually run AI data centers 24/7**. Wind and solar can't do it; the grid can't reliably do it; nuclear can. Constellation gets a guaranteed revenue stream and a strategic technology partner. Google locks in power at predictable rates that insulate it from electricity price inflation driven by... AI power demand. The circular logic is poetic and real.

**What's next**: Every nuclear utility in North America is getting repriced. Watch **VST** (Vistra Energy), **NRG**, **ETR** (Entergy), and **DUK** (Duke Energy) for follow-on deals. The bottleneck isn't capital — it's nuclear capacity, regulatory approval for plant life extensions, and skilled labor. This is a multi-year structural theme, not a one-day trade.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**The central tension today**: Markets printed record highs Tuesday on AI optimism and 29.5% expected Q3 EPS growth — then immediately reversed Wednesday as 30Y yields hit 5.70% (highest since 2002) and Brent crossed $100. This is the fundamental contradiction of the 2026 bull market: exceptional earnings growth is colliding with a cost of capital regime that should compress multiples. The market keeps choosing growth over rates. Eventually that math breaks.

**Dominant Themes This Week**

1. **AI Power Infrastructure as an investable theme**: The CEG/Google nuclear deal is not a one-off. The hyperscalers have a structural power problem. The clean power grid cannot keep up with data center demand. Nuclear is the only answer at scale. This is the next multi-year trade after AI chips — and it's earlier innings than NVDA was in late 2023.

2. **The Fed's tightrope**: First hike since 2023 in September. Now oil above $100 is re-inflating the CPI pipeline while French credit stress and a 29K jobs print argue for a pause. The minutes today will reveal how divided the Committee actually was. The bond market is pricing more trouble (30Y at 5.70%) than the equity market is acknowledging.

3. **Mag 7 fatigue at the margin**: META down 2.2%, NVDA flat, TSLA grinding lower. The Mag 7 accounts for 34.9% of S&P 500. If this cohort consolidates or rotates, the index math gets ugly fast. So far it's orderly.

4. **Steel and industrials softness**: Worthington's EPS miss is a datapoint in a larger industrial slowdown story. Capex is flowing into AI/data center but away from traditional manufacturing. Watch ISM Manufacturing; it's been sub-50 for several months.

### Risks to Current Consensus

- **Consensus**: Fed done hiking, soft landing achieved, AI earnings justify current multiples.
- **What could break this**: (1) FOMC minutes signal more hikes are in play → bond market reprices → equity multiple compression accelerates; (2) Brent stays above $100 through year-end → October CPI re-accelerates → Fed forced to hike again; (3) French sovereign stress spreads to Italy → European bank contagion → risk-off globally; (4) Mag 7 earnings season disappoints on guidance (capex looks too high, monetization too slow).

### What the Market Is Not Pricing That It Should Be

**The 30Y at 5.70% is incompatible with the S&P at current multiples, and the market is choosing to ignore it.** At 5.70% on the 30Y, the theoretical equity risk premium for the S&P 500 at ~22x forward earnings is essentially zero. You're being paid nothing to own equity risk over government bonds. The last time this logic was inverted was 2000. Markets can stay irrational for a while — but this gap will eventually resolve, either via rates falling or equity multiples compressing.

The second under-priced risk: **oil above $100 is not a supply story, it's a geopolitical story**. A geopolitical supply disruption is non-linear. If Middle East tensions escalate, $120–130 Brent is on the table. That is not priced.
