## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve
Kevin Warsh took over as Chair in May 2026 and has hammered the "price stability first" message at every opportunity. He stepped back from the extended dovish 2023–2025 pause and delivered a 25 bps hike in September, lifting the Fed funds target to **3.75%–4.00%** — the first hike in more than three years.

The **FOMC meeting is October 27–28.** Fed officials are in their blackout period as of approximately October 17, meaning no public guidance before the decision. Market consensus is strongly for a **hold** — roughly 15–35% hike probability depending on the source (CME FedWatch, Forbes models diverge). The bias is clearly for a December hike if the data continues to print hot.

The reason for the December lean: September CPI came in at **3.7% YoY** — hotter than the 3.4% prior month and above consensus. The Cleveland Fed nowcast is tracking October at **3.77% YoY**. With oil spiking to $110 this week, November CPI is now at serious risk of printing 4.0%+ — which would materially change the December meeting calculus.

**Critical structural point the market is underpricing:** The Fed's own September projections show no return to 2% inflation until **2029**. That's not a near-term policy path problem; that's a structural repricing of the long end. If you're not thinking about where the 10Y goes in a world where the Fed remains above neutral for 3+ more years, you should be.

### Central Banks (Global)
- **ECB:** Holding. European inflation is decelerating and ECB is on an extended pause. The strong EUR/USD 1.12 level is partly a function of ECB policy being less hawkish than the Fed-implied differential would suggest.
- **BOJ:** Still a wildcard with USD/JPY at 158. Governor Ueda has been threading the needle between yield curve control normalization and not sparking a yen crisis. The next BOJ meeting (late October) is increasingly live.
- **PBOC:** Quiet amid trade truce extension, but CNH stability near 7.11 suggests managed float is holding.

### Geopolitical Developments
**Middle East / Oil Supply:**
This is the number one market risk right now. UKMTO documented 12 Iranian attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters between October 10–17. Houthi forces in Yemen have intensified strikes on Saudi infrastructure while US–Iran negotiations are stalled. This is a genuine supply disruption risk, not just premium noise. $110 Brent with those attack numbers is actually *not* an overreaction.

**Trump/Russia Sanctions:**
Trump waived key Russia oil sanctions with a 60-day waiver — described by Sec. Rubio as a temporary easing of restrictions that could have imposed 100% tariffs on countries importing Russian oil. Paradoxically, this is slightly bearish for oil long-term (marginal Russian supply back on market) but the market read it as geopolitical unpredictability = buy oil. Watch for reversal if the waiver is extended.

**US–China Trade:**

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**Theme 1: The Rate Reprice Is Not Done.**
The 10Y at 5.09% with CPI running at 3.7% and an oil spike in progress is a market screaming that the "soft landing" narrative may need to be revised. The Fed is officially in a higher-for-longer mode (December hike consensus at 63–89% depending on the source), and Warsh has no political incentive to pivot. The 5.25% 10Y was where something broke in 2023 — we are 16 bps away from that level with materially worse starting valuations on equity.

**Theme 2: Rotation Out of AI Infrastructure, Into AI Monetization.**
This is the most important sector call right now. The chipmakers (NVDA, AMD, MU) are being repriced lower not because AI is failing but because the infrastructure investment required to *enable* AI is capital-intensive in a high-rate world. Meanwhile, companies that can monetize AI at the application layer — META (Muse), MSFT (Azure AI revenue), AAPL (AI device upgrade cycle) — are outperforming. The market is moving from "build the rails" to "who profits from the train running."

**Theme 3: Energy Is Back as a Macro Factor.**
$110 Brent was not consensus two weeks ago. The Strait of Hormuz attack frequency is alarming — 12 incidents in 7 days is not noise. If oil holds above $105 into November CPI, the December FOMC calculus changes materially. Energy sector (XOM, CVX, SLB, PSX) is the obvious beneficiary and is acting like it — Dow outperforming on energy weight while Nasdaq craters is the spread to watch.

**Theme 4: Banks Are Not Your Friend.**
JPMorgan missing, Schwab down on revenue dynamics, Truist posting a 30% revenue miss — the banking sector is navigating a difficult NIM/credit demand matrix. The beats are all cost-driven. When banks can't grow revenue in a 5% rate environment, it means loan demand is weak, which is a leading recession indicator. Not crying wolf — but this pattern bears watching.

**Theme 5: Small/Mid Cap Under Pressure.**
Higher-for-longer hits smaller companies harder (more floating rate debt, less pricing power). The Dow holding up while Nasdaq craters reflects large-cap / defensive rotation. If the 10Y goes to 5.25%+, the Russell 2000 and small-cap growth will reprice aggressively.

### What the Market Is Not Pricing (But Should Be)

1. **Oil staying above $100 long-term.** The Strait of Hormuz risk is being treated as temporary premium. If it's structural (ongoing Iran proxy conflict), the commodity inflation transmission into CPI is a 3–6 month lag problem that means 2027 monetary policy is much tighter than projected.

2. **A BOJ surprise.** USD/JPY at 158 with Japanese real yields still deeply negative is an unstable equilibrium. A BOJ policy adjustment at the late-October meeting — even a modest tweak — creates significant yen carry unwind. That's a global liquidity event, not a Japan problem.

3. **Mag 7 earnings disappointment risk.** Tesla reports next Wednesday. If TSLA misses AND cuts guidance, the stock re-rates sharply (PE 327 → what's fair in a recession?). Cloud reports from AMZN and MSFT the week after are even more systemically important. If cloud revenue decelerates on the back of corporate IT budget cuts, the entire AI buildout narrative faces a fundamental challenge.

4. **The CCC/CCCS going-private at $6.1B signals PE sees insurance-tech as undervalued.** The public market isn't pricing the PE bid premium across the insurance-tech/fintech vertical.
