## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve — First Hike Since 2023
The September 16 FOMC meeting (last week) was the central macro event of the month. The Committee **raised the fed funds rate by 25bps to 3.75%-4.00%** in a unanimous vote — the first increase since 2023. Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** stated inflation has been "too high for too long" and that "today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." 

The new dot plot:
- 2026 GDP: 2.3% (revised up from 2.2%)
- 2026 unemployment: 4.1% (revised down 0.2pp)
- Two additional hikes projected through mid-2027 (December 2026 and March 2027)
- Terminal rate implied: ~4.375%

**What this means:** Warsh is not Powell. He was always the hawkish hawk — this is a Fed Chair who genuinely believes inflation expectations need anchoring even at the cost of some growth. The unanimous vote is the signal. There is no dovish dissent. The December hike is nearly fully priced.

**The hidden risk:** The Fed is hiking into a falling oil price environment. If Brent falls to $85 and CPI drops toward 2.5% by Q1 2027, Warsh will be hiking into disinflation — and the market will figure that out well before the Fed does. Watch the 2Y yield — if it starts to roll over despite hike talk, the bond market is calling the Fed's bluff.

### Bank of Japan

## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### The Meta Muse Catalyst — CPU Demand Thesis Is the Story of the Week

Meta's Muse personal AI agent hit the #1 spot on the Apple App Store free charts and has now held that position for **three consecutive days**. This is not just a download story — it's a fundamental shift in the chip demand narrative that deserves a proper walkthrough.

**Why Muse moves chips:**
- AI agents perform multi-step, multi-tool tasks continuously — requiring CPUs to coordinate models, applications, and data
- Server-side CPU requirements for agentic AI are rising by "at least 4x" per workload vs. simple query-response AI
- The bull thesis for the past two years was GPU-centric (training, inference). The Muse adoption curve introduces a **CPU/edge computing demand leg** that is entirely additive
- Intel and Qualcomm are the most direct beneficiaries; Arm Holdings is the architecture that underpins nearly all of them

This is the cycle catalyst that had been missing since the initial generative AI wave slowed in late 2025. The key question: is Muse a sustained platform or a trending app? Three days at #1 says platform. The chip market is treating it as platform.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You
The market is simultaneously pricing in: (1) a Fed rate-hiking cycle, (2) a diplomatic resolution to the Iran war, (3) an AI agent super-cycle, and (4) a Nasdaq at all-time highs. That's a lot of good news in one tape. It is not internally inconsistent, but it leaves almost no margin for disappointment on any of the four fronts.

**The dominant theme this week is the CPU/agentic AI demand rerating.** The Muse catalyst has done something important: it created a consumer-observable data point for AI agent adoption. When the #1 free iPhone app for three days running is an AI agent, the demand story is no longer theoretical. The market is re-pricing the entire CPU value chain — ARM, Intel, Qualcomm, and now through them, SanDisk for AI storage — as infrastructure rather than cyclical hardware. That's a multiple-expansion event, and it's still in early innings.

**The oil/Iran story is the tail risk the bulls don't want to think about.** Trump's UNGA signaling is dovish. But Trump's track record on Iran negotiations is volatile. Every prior "we're close to a deal" moment has been followed by escalation. The market has already priced in $95 oil based on diplomatic optimism. If the UNGA meeting doesn't happen, or if it happens and fails, Brent goes back above $100 before the week is out, and the inflation-relief/tech-multiple-expansion thesis partially unwinds.

**Banks are the canary.** The -1.8% move in banks on a day when S&P was +1.49% is the clearest expression of what the smart money thinks the Fed hike means: not NIM expansion, but credit stress ahead. If this rotation persists — banks down, tech up, in a rising rate environment — it signals the market is pricing a "soft landing for tech, hard landing for credit" scenario. That is historically an unstable equilibrium.

**The Fed/BOJ divergence is a ticking clock.** BOJ at 1.25%, Fed at 3.875% (on its way to 4.375%). USD/JPY at 157 means the yen carry trade is alive and well. The unwind of this trade historically produces violent cross-asset moves: yen rallies sharply, USD/JPY drops 15-20 pts in weeks, US Treasuries sell off (Japanese investors repatriate), and risk assets get hit. No one knows the trigger, but the conditions are present. Size your yen exposure accordingly.

**What the market is not pricing:**
1. **Failed UNGA diplomacy on Iran.** The entire oil/inflation relief narrative rests on Trump's UNGA meeting happening and succeeding. The market is not pricing the alternative.
2. **Fed overtightening into a disinflation.** If oil falls to $80 by Q1 2027, CPI could be at 2.0-2.5% when the Fed is still hiking. Warsh hiking into disinflation would be the first major policy error of his tenure.
3. **Yen carry unwind.** The volatility event of 2026 that hasn't happened yet.
4. **China's response to Alibaba capex.** Beijing-driven chip investment could create supply chain responses from the US (more export controls) that disrupt the chip rally.
5. **Meta Muse monetization proof.** Being #1 on the App Store and being a revenue engine are different things. Meta has never successfully monetized its consumer AI products at scale. The $200B market cap addition is predicated on Muse converting to revenue — that's a thesis, not a fact.
