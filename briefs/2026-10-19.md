## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Trump Russia Sanctions Waiver — The Weekend Bombshell
The most important macro development of the weekend: the Trump administration on October 16 suspended sanctions and tariffs on Russian fuel exports under the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act — a law that had been set to take effect October 18. The 60-day waiver ties to ongoing Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations (Trump's framing: "decision was simple, as I was negotiating peace"). Secretary of State Rubio gets 60 days to assess economic impact.

This is a meaningful policy shift. The market's interpretation is that Russian crude and products flow back into global markets gradually, easing the Hormuz-driven supply panic. But analysts are right to be skeptical of the supply response: Russian infrastructure hasn't been maintained, and sanctions have kept Russian crude at steep discounts to Brent. This may be more about perception than actual near-term barrel flows. The risk is if the peace talks collapse — we're right back to $110+.

## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### The Context: Worst Week Since Liberation Day
Last week's selloff wasn't a one-day event. Chip stocks fell for **8 consecutive sessions** going into Friday. Intel lost >20% from its June peak. AMD shed 5%+ on October 14 alone. The trigger was a market repricing of AI capex sustainability — with PPI at 6.3% and the 10Y at 5.08%, the question is simple: can hyperscalers keep spending $300B+ annually on AI infrastructure when their cost of capital is rising? The "AI debt trade" thesis (borrow cheap, build data centers, monetize later) looks shakier at 5% rates than it did at 3%.

### Semis — The Intel Upgrade Story
**INTC** is the stand-out name today. JPMorgan's Harlan Sur upgraded from Underweight to Neutral (target maintained at $85) after Intel fell ~36% from its June peak. Sur's key thesis:
1. **Agentic AI workloads lean CPU-heavy** — as inference moves to real-time agent tasks, the relative importance of high-quality x86 cores may inflect.
2. **Execution under Lip-Bu Tan**: Foundry reorganization showing early progress; Intel 18A process yield improving.
3. **Near-term share loss in server CPUs is priced in**.

Critically, Sur is NOT upgrading because the business is fixed. He's upgrading because the risk/reward improved after the stock priced in a very bad outcome. Still expects a few more quarters of server CPU share loss to AMD. Price target of $85 is below today's price (~$93), which is an unusual dynamic — the street is actually trading through the analyst's target. That's either a setup for target raises, or a warning that the bounce overshoots.

**AMD** (+3-4%): Beneficiary of the INTC upgrade narrative and the general AI chip recovery. AMD's MI300X continues to take share in GPU inferencing against NVDA — but the NVDA moat via software (CUDA) is still the dominant story.

**TSMC (TSM)**: Strong Q3 — profit up 68.7% YoY — but US ADR shares fell 1.8% on Friday. The paradox: TSMC's results confirm AI chip demand is real and robust, but the market is selling on the "everything priced in" logic. Capacity is genuinely tight. NVDA, Apple, and AMD are all fighting for 3nm/2nm allocation. TSMC reports Taiwan numbers Thursday — the US market gets a look.

**MU** (+4%): Micron recovering with the broader chip sector. The company sits in the sweet spot of the AI infrastructure build — HBM3E demand for NVDA's GPU stacks. Samsung has reportedly begun working with TSMC to produce the base die of next-gen HBM on TSMC's 3nm node — this is Nvidia pushing suppliers toward TSMC to maintain quality control and potentially shift leverage away from any single memory supplier.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**The AI Capex Debt Trade is under review, not dead.** Last week's selloff was not random; it was a coherent repricing of a specific thesis: borrow cheap, build the AI stack, monetize later. At 5% 10Y yields and 6.3% PPI, the "borrow cheap" part of that equation no longer exists. The market spent all of last week stress-testing whether the AI trade still works in a high-rate, high-inflation environment. Today's bounce suggests the answer is "yes, but at a lower multiple." NVDA trading at 35x forward earnings instead of 45x is still NVDA. The Datadog upgrade thesis (AI token consumption +50% QoQ) is a real data point that the underlying demand is accelerating even if the stock was priced for perfection.

**It's Black Monday Anniversary — and That's All It Is.** The social media panic over the weekend, comparing today to October 19, 1987, was noise. The macro parallels are superficial: 1987 was a portfolio insurance feedback loop, not an AI capex debate. The market bouncing today actually strengthens the long-term bullish case for disciplined dip-buyers. That said, anniversary volatility is real and options positioning around this date is historically elevated — watch VIX intraday.

**Oil is Not a Solved Problem.** Brent down $2 on the Russia sanctions waiver is a relief, not a resolution. Hormuz throughput running at 40% of historical levels is a structural energy shock. Russian supply restoration at scale takes 6–12 months minimum even with a full sanctions lift. The market will realize this within a week or two unless Hormuz disruptions actually end. If you're short crude on this news alone, you're early.

**France is the Canary in the European Coal Mine.** The French protests go beyond fiscal policy — they reflect a political fragmentation that's been building since the 2025 elections. If OAT-Bund spreads widen materially (currently ~80bps, watch for 100+), the ECB faces a policy constraint that limits its ability to respond to European slowdown. This is not priced in equity markets outside France.

**Semiconductor Crowding is the Biggest Risk.** Hedge fund Mag 7 exposure at the highest since early 2022 is not a bullish signal on its own — it's a position that needs a constant stream of good news to sustain itself. Meta and Tesla reports on Wednesday are not just earnings events; they're tests of whether the crowded trade narrative holds. If Meta misses on monetization or guides conservatively on AI infrastructure costs, the unwind could be brutal.

**What the Market Is Not Pricing:**
- Indonesia EM contagion risk (MSCI warning is real)
- US fiscal deficit at $2T becoming a bond market story (net supply of USTs is enormous at 5% yields)
- French political risk spilling beyond equities into OAT spreads
- The possibility that the Russia sanctions waiver accelerates ceasefire talks in ways that reshape the entire geopolitical premium baked into energy, defense, and Eastern European markets
