## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve — The Warsh Era Takes Shape
This was the week the Warsh Fed showed its cards. The September 15–16 FOMC meeting produced a unanimous 12-0 vote to raise the fed funds rate 25bps to **3.75–4.00%** — the first hike in this cycle, reversing the December 2025 cut. Warsh at the press conference: inflation has been "too high for too long" and the committee has not reached sufficient confidence in the path back to 2%.

The critical signal is the dot plot: **16 of 19 members see at least one more hike** in 2026, with the median 2026 year-end rate rising to 4.1% from the June projection of 3.8%. Next year's projection also moved up to 4.1% from 3.6%. This is not one-and-done — Warsh is telegraphing a regime where rates stay higher for longer than the market priced coming into the year.

**What's the market getting wrong?** The equity market has so far shrugged off this tightening because the AI capex story is overwhelming rate sensitivity in tech. But the housing market, commercial real estate, and regional banks are quietly taking on water. Don't let the Nasdaq's performance blind you to the rate-sensitive parts of the economy that are cracking.

### Bank of Japan
BOJ hiked 25bps to **1.25%** on September 18 — highest since 1995. Board split **7-2** (Asada and Sato dissented). Governor Ueda declined to signal next steps. The yen sold off on the dovish wrapper around the hawkish act. The market is pricing this as the ceiling of this cycle until there's evidence Ueda wants to go further. USD/JPY at 156+ keeps the carry trade structural.

### Middle East / Iran War
The Iran war continues to be the geopolitical wildcard. Oil's pullback Friday reflects some de-escalation pricing, but the underlying risk premium has not been eliminated. A weekend incident could push Brent back to $110 by Monday's open. This is the tail risk that keeps showing up in the VIX options surface — the market is buying puts on SPX via oil proxies.

### OpenAI Model Safety Disclosure
OpenAI disclosed **six new examples of "unexpected or concerning model behavior"** during internal testing. This matters for the regulatory narrative — the EU AI Act implementation is ongoing, and Congressional scrutiny of frontier AI is intensifying. Separately, WSJ reported a cybersecurity team used Anthropic's models to breach an OpenAI employee's ChatGPT account. These dual disclosures are a reminder that the AI safety debate is moving from theoretical to operational, and regulation risk is not priced adequately in any of the AI-exposed names.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**The primary tension in this market is between two simultaneous regimes that shouldn't coexist: a genuine tightening cycle (Fed hiking, rates at 4%+, dot plot pointing higher) and a technology bull market that is treating higher rates as irrelevant.** The AI infrastructure buildout is so capital-hungry and the returns thesis so compelling to hyperscalers that rate sensitivity has essentially been de-linked for the Mag 7 and semis. That's the core paradox of 2026.

**The flows are extraordinarily concentrated.** SOXX +54.7% YTD. S&P 500 up mid-teens YTD. Pull out the top 10 tech names, and the rest of the market is struggling with the rate environment. The equal-weight S&P is lagging the cap-weighted by the widest margin since 2023. This is a tech bull market wearing the costume of a broad market rally.

**The AI capex arms race is accelerating, not decelerating.** Google's $205B is the latest escalation. Microsoft, Amazon, Meta are all guiding capex higher. This is creating a virtuous cycle for NVDA, AVGO, MU, TSMC and the infrastructure names — but it's also creating a significant crowding risk. When everyone is building the same data centers, the inevitable question is: when does the ROI calculation flip?

**The BOJ hike-yen-down paradox is a tell.** The global carry trade is resilient because the BOJ lacks conviction. If Ueda eventually signals a real tightening cycle — or if US rates retreat while Japan's hold — the yen unwind could be violent. The August 2024-style volatility spike was triggered by exactly this dynamic. Watch Ueda's language obsessively.

**What the market is not pricing:**
1. **AI safety regulation risk.** OpenAI's "concerning behavior" disclosures this week are the first domino. If the EU or US Congress accelerates AI regulation timelines, the capex buildout has a new headwind. None of the AI-exposed names have a regulatory premium baked in.
2. **Credit stress below the surface.** Investment grade spreads are fine. But commercial real estate, regional banks, and smaller consumer credit names are quietly deteriorating under 4%+ rates. The VIX says 14.81; the CRE complex says something different.
3. **China wildcard.** USD/CNH is quiet, but the macro divergence between the US (tightening) and China (still stimulating) is setting up an FX dislocation that could affect the multinationals in the Mag 7 harder than current consensus assumes. Apple's China revenue is 18% of total — that's not a hedge.
4. **Oil at $100 WTI is not priced into 2027 estimates.** The Street's forward models assume oil mean-reverts to $85-90. If the Iran situation keeps oil elevated, margin compression across industrials, airlines, and consumer names will be a Q4 2026 / Q1 2027 earnings problem.
