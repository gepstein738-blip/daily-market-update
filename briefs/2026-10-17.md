## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### The Dominant Narrative: AI Financing Crisis Meets Hot Inflation

This week's selloff was not random. The thesis that drove chip stocks to a 68% YTD gain through mid-September — unlimited AI spending, insatiable GPU demand, infinite data center buildout — ran into two walls simultaneously:

1. **Financing costs:** At 5% on the 10-year, the massive debt required to fund AI infrastructure capex is expensive. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said outright that financing could be a bigger challenge to AI expansion than chip shortages or power constraints. When a CEO of a $700B company says that publicly, listen.

2. **Capital markets reality check:** CoreWeave's bonds going distressed is the tell. This is not a mature, cash-flowing business; it's a highly-leveraged AI infrastructure bet. The equity may have gotten ahead of the credit, and the credit is now correcting.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**1. The AI trade is not dead, but it's bifurcating.** The names that monetize AI through software and recurring revenue (CRM, NOW, CRWD, PLTR) are getting upgraded. The names that require massive capital expenditure and carry the financing risk (CRWV, the chip equipment cycle bets, pure-play infrastructure) are getting cut. The market is sorting through who owns the AI toll road and who borrowed to build it.

**2. Inflation is not over.** September CPI +3.7% YoY was not a blip — the Fed hiked in September for the first time since 2023, and the October meeting is live. Energy-driven inflation is regime-change inflation: it doesn't respond to rate hikes, it responds to geopolitics. With Iran in the mix and Brent at $105, every single business with fuel, shipping, or energy exposure is under margin pressure heading into Q4 guidance season.

**3. The tech selloff is credit-led, not equity-led.** This is the most important nuance to understand. The fact that CoreWeave bonds went distressed *before* the equity woke up is the sequence you see in credit crises, not growth-stock corrections. If high-yield spreads are widening in AI infrastructure specifically, that is a structural warning about how AI buildout is being financed. This is not a de-rating of future earnings — it's a re-rating of the risk-free rate applied to speculative, cash-flow-negative businesses.

**4. The Magnificent 7 is no longer one trade.** Nvidia is holding. Microsoft and Alphabet are holding. Tesla reversed. Apple is drifting. Meta is unclear. The era of the basket trade is over — you need to own the name, not the acronym.

**5. Regional banks are not the 2023 crisis redux — yet.** The EPS beat + revenue miss pattern at SCHW, PNC, and Regions looks like margin compression, not solvency risk. But if the 10-year holds above 5% and loan growth stalls, the unrealized portfolio loss problem from 2023 is still sitting on balance sheets. This is a slow-moving risk that the equity market is not focused on.

### Risks to Current Consensus

- **Consensus:** Fed holds October 27–28. Chip selloff is a correction within a bull trend. AI spending continues regardless of financing costs.
- **Risks not priced:** (1) Warsh speaks hawkish in Bangkok → October hike repriced to 40%+ odds, 10-year re-tests 5.30%. (2) Anthropic IPO misses its $2T target → marks down private AI company valuations and triggers secondary selling in public AI names. (3) CRWV bond distress spreads to other AI infrastructure borrowers (Crusoe, Lambda Labs, SambaNova) → credit channel tightens for sector. (4) Delta's guidance is the first of many airline/logistics guidance cuts → inflation + fuel = Q4 earnings risk across transports. (5) Indonesia MSCI reclassification triggers EM contagion → broader risk-off into Monday.

### What the Market Is Sleeping On

**The Broadcom call.** When Hock Tan publicly says financing is the binding constraint on AI expansion, the market prices it as one executive's opinion. It is actually a read-through for every leveraged AI infrastructure bet in the ecosystem. Broadcom has the best visibility into hyperscaler custom silicon demand of any public company outside NVDA and TSMC. If he is signaling that financing > supply as the constraint, the bull models that assume unconstrained demand growth are wrong.
