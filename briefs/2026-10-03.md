## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The Jobs Report Is the Story

Friday's September NFP print was a genuine shock: **+29,000 jobs** vs. consensus **+84,000**. Unemployment rose to **4.2%** (partly labor force re-entry — don't dismiss the rise, but don't over-weight it either). Average hourly earnings +3.0% YoY, flat-to-soft on growth.

**Revisions compounded the miss:** July revised to **-10,000** (was +21,000); August revised to **+133,000** (was +162,000). Two-month downward revision: **-60,000**. This is not a one-month data aberration — the labor market has been softening on a rolling basis for three consecutive months.

**The Fed Repricing Was Violent:**
- One week ago: **>70% probability** of a Fed rate hike at October 27–28 FOMC, following Governor Barr's hawkish commentary and an inflation print described as the highest in nearly four years.
- Friday post-data: **82.8% probability of a hold** (CME FedWatch). October rate hike odds: **17%** — effectively off the table.
- This is one of the fastest shifts in FOMC probability pricing since the March 2023 banking crisis. December is now the next live meeting, and even that is getting questioned.

**The contradition the market is choosing to ignore:** Average hourly earnings are still running +3.0% YoY. The pre-jobs inflation print was called "the highest in nearly four years." You can't simultaneously have the worst jobs report in years AND sticky inflation and call it a soft landing — that combination is textbook stagflation. Markets traded Friday's data as benign demand softness. They may be right. But the inflation piece doesn't disappear.

**Key Fed Voices:**
- **NY Fed President John Williams** (pre-jobs, September): "One further upward adjustment may be appropriate late this year, but there is no urgency." Williams is the most important non-Chair voice at the Fed. His framing now looks more prescient than hawkish. Watch for any Williams remarks next week — his tone will set expectations for the October 28 press conference.
- **Fed Governor Barr** had been hawkish going into Friday; his September comments partially catalyzed the pre-jobs 70% hike odds. Silence from him post-data Friday. Watch for a walk-back.
- **FOMC: October 27–28.** Powell press conference October 28. Between now and then: one more CPI (October 15), one more PPI, and one more initial claims reading.

**G7 SPR Release — 100M Barrels:** Agreed at US request. Timing and logistics TBD; the market sold oil immediately. ## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

**Theme 1: The Soft Landing Trade Is Back — For Now**
Twenty-nine thousand jobs is not a number a hiking Fed ignores. The market has decisively repriced October off the table and S&P at 7,722 with rate hike risk collapsing is textbook duration expansion. Tech/growth outperformed Friday; defensives and energy lagged. Breadth was good. Volume was solid. If you were bearishly positioned, Friday was a painful day.

**The crux:** Average hourly earnings are still running +3.0% YoY. The pre-jobs inflation print was described as the highest reading in nearly four years. A weakening labor market with sticky inflation is *stagflation*, not a soft landing. Markets chose to trade the miss as benign demand softness. They may be right — labor markets can weaken without inflation accelerating. But the market is not holding both facts in view simultaneously; it is choosing the comfortable narrative.

**Theme 2: AI Infrastructure Capex Has No Ceiling in Sight**
Google CFO on a $195-205B annual capex budget: "Demand still outpaces investment." Nvidia at $5.67T market cap all-time high. Google Cloud +82% YoY. AMD calling for $2 trillion in compute spend by 2030. AI data centers are now being launched into orbit (Project Suncatcher). The beneficiaries of this spending are increasingly well-defined: NVDA, AVGO, MRVL, MPWR, COHR, TER, NTAP, HPE, VRT. Every weak day in these names on broad market volatility is a buying opportunity until the capex narrative cracks — and the narrative is getting more aggressive, not less.

**Theme 3: Cerebras vs. Nvidia Is the Most Important Relative Performance Chart**
CBRS at a post-IPO low, NVDA at an all-time high. The AI chip market is not a rising tide — it is winner-take-most, and Nvidia keeps winning. Every dollar of AI inference spend that runs on Nvidia rather than a challenger is another brick in the competitive moat. Watch CBRS/NVDA relative performance weekly — it is the single best real-time read on whether alternative AI chip architecture theses have any validity.

**Theme 4: Microsoft Is the Mag 7 Tell for Q3 Earnings Season**
MSFT down 17.4% YTD while NVDA is at record highs. The market is expressly skeptical that Azure's AI monetization justifies the capex spending. MSFT's mid-October earnings print is the single most consequential event of Q3 reporting season. A beat-and-raise on Azure AI revenue could be the most violent single-day rally in any Mag 7 name this year. A miss or soft guidance could drag the entire growth complex.

**1. The Oil Refill Trade.** G7 released 100M bbl and markets sold crude. SPRs must be refilled. If OPEC+ announces cuts at the November meeting — and history strongly suggests they would respond to a coordinated SPR release with production discipline — WTI goes back to $96+ before year-end. Energy is being slept on. The options market in XOM and CVX is pricing benign; that's where the risk/reward is asymmetric.

If November payrolls bounce to +120K+ and the October CPI print (due in two weeks) shows inflation accelerating again, October's dovish reprice partially reverses. The 10-year was down ~10–12 bps Friday; if inflation re-accelerates, that move gives back. Long-duration positioning that went on Friday is running a real risk of a two-week reversal.

If CBRS breaks $160, it becomes a momentum sell and the entire "alternative AI chip" VC cohort (several companies still private) gets repriced in private markets.
