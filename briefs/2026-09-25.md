## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The Fed: Kevin Warsh's First Hike

The FOMC delivered a 25bps hike at its September meeting — the first tightening move since July 2023 — bringing the target range to **3.75%–4.00%**. This is Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's signature moment: the former Fed governor who spent years warning about the Bernanke-era "financial repression" is now in the chair and has pulled the trigger. Sixteen of eighteen FOMC members project at least one more hike before year-end; four see two additional increases.

**The September SEP dot plot:**
- End-2026 median: 4.1% (one more hike priced in)
- 2027: stays at 4.1%
- Longer run: eventually falls to 3.9%

**NY Fed President John Williams** (speech Thursday, London, National Institute of Economic and Social Research): Said explicitly it is "reasonable" to expect another rate hike by year-end. Added that explicit forward guidance "belongs to a prior era" — echoing Warsh's own stated philosophy of no pre-commitments. Market odds for a November or December 25bps hike sitting at ~70%.

What Warsh has done that Powell never fully could: separated the rate path from the communication game. The market can no longer fade the Fed by reading between the lines of press conference non-answers. This is a more hawkish regime on process alone.

**August Durable Goods Orders** (released today):
- Headline: Flat MoM vs. -0.4% consensus → slight beat
- Core capex (non-defense, ex-aircraft): **+0.9%** MoM — strong; businesses still investing despite rate pressure
- Positive sub-components: computers/electronics +3.1%, primary metals +1.1%, defense aircraft +5.9%
- Negative: transportation -0.2%, motor vehicles/parts -0.6%

The core capex print is the number that matters. Business investment holding up while consumers are squeezed by fuel costs and higher mortgage rates argues for sticky inflation and a Fed that stays higher for longer. This data is NOT consistent with a November pause.

### Iran / Strait of Hormuz — The Key Macro Variable of 2026

The background: The US-Israel coalition launched military action against Iran's nuclear program in spring 2026. Iran retaliated by restricting Strait of Hormuz navigation — through which >20% of global oil trade flows. JPMorgan estimates total Middle East oil flows at 17M bbl/day, ~70% of the 2025 average. Brent went from ~$79 pre-conflict to $106+ at peak in early September.

**Today's catalyst for the oil pullback:** Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz and restarting nuclear talks with the US within **7 days** — if Washington accepts Tehran's conditions. The specific conditions are not yet public but the directional signal is positive. CNBC reported US and Iran are "considering a phased agreement."

Oil dropped -2.18% to $92.55 on this news. The setup is: if talks succeed, Brent probably returns to $75-80 relatively quickly (supply premium unwinds); if talks fail or collapse, we're back toward $100+. The asymmetry is wide. This is the single most important geopolitical variable in global markets right now.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### Theme 1: The Iran Option — Oil Market's Binary Moment

The Strait of Hormuz story has dominated macro for three months. Oil went from $79 → $106+ → $92.55 today. The Iran FM's 7-day proposal is the market's clearest off-ramp yet from the energy shock. If a phased agreement materializes, the supply premium unwinds and you could see Brent at $78-82 within 30 days. That would take 50-75bps off core PCE expectations and potentially remove the case for the Fed's December hike entirely.

The risk: Iran has made these overtures before. The conditions attached are unknown. If this is a stall tactic or if the US rejects the terms, oil reverses sharply. The tape today is cautiously optimistic — pricing partial probability of success. Position sizing accordingly: long producers are crowded, short crude has been painful, and this setup has a wide binary outcome.

**What the market isn't pricing:** If Hormuz reopens AND Iran nuclear talks restart simultaneously, this is potentially the biggest macro tailwind of 2026. Lower oil + potential sanctions relief on Iranian crude supply + Fed pause = risk-on rally of significant magnitude. The odds are not high, but the market is assigning near-zero probability.

### Theme 2: The Fed's Credibility Reset Under Warsh

The market got used to Powell-era forward guidance — every move was telegraphed 60 days in advance. Warsh has explicitly ended that. Williams echoing "forward guidance belongs to a prior era" yesterday confirms the regime change. This means: every data print matters again. Every Fed speaker matters again. The two-day reactions to CPI day are structural, not temporary.

The 70% odds of a November/December hike embedded in the market today — that's the new normal. It won't get resolved until the October CPI print (November 14). Between now and then, every energy price, every wages number, every services inflation sub-component is a Fed meeting in microcosm.

The 10Y at 5.19% is pricing one more hike AND a higher neutral rate AND elevated term premium from fiscal deficits. The 2s10s at +29bps (dis-inverting from -100bps) is the bond market telling you this is a genuine tightening cycle, not a fake-out. Fixed income is doing what it's supposed to do.

### Theme 3: Mag 7 Cheapest in a Decade — Rotation or Value Trap?

Morgan Stanley's note that Mag 7 premium to S&P is now ~10% (from 30%+) is the most consequential fundamental call of late 2026. Two interpretations:

**Bull case:** These are still the highest-quality compounders on earth. Revenue growth remains 15-20%+ at scale. AI infrastructure spend will monetize. At 10% premium to the index vs. historical 30%, you're getting the best businesses in the world at the cheapest relative price since 2013. Buy.

**Bear case:** Rate environment justifies the compression. At 5.19% risk-free, you need to discount future cash flows harder. AI capex is consuming capital faster than it's generating returns. The 10% premium is still 10% too much if we're headed to 4.25% Fed funds. The de-rating continues.

The honest answer: Mag 7 multiple compression was long overdue and is largely appropriate given rates. The trade is not "buy the dip blindly" — it's to be selective. NVDA (AI infrastructure irreplaceable) and GOOGL (AI search moat validated) are structurally different from MSFT and META which are investing ahead of monetization. AAPL is a product cycle bet. TSLA is a conviction call at any price.

### Theme 4: AI Infrastructure — Public Market Skepticism vs. Private Market Conviction

The disconnect is stark: public Mag 7 multiples are compressing on AI capex concerns, while BlackRock is writing a $25B check for data centers in Asia. Institutional investors with 10-year return horizons are buying the infrastructure because the hyperscaler demand commitments are contracted out 5-7 years. Public equity investors are marking AI to quarterly results and getting nervous about the capex.

This gap doesn't usually persist indefinitely. Either the public market comes to accept the infrastructure thesis (Mag 7 multiples re-expand) or the private deals turn out to have been bought at the top (data center oversupply 2029+). The answer will be determined by whether large language models continue their capability improvement curve and whether enterprises actually convert AI spending into productivity gains.

### Theme 5: Bond Market Vigilantism Returns

The 30Y at 5.49% is the policy transmission mechanism that matters. No one is buying a home at these rates. Every leveraged buyout model needs to be rebuilt. Corporate debt refinancing at 300bps+ over where it was issued in 2021 is a genuine earnings headwind. The Treasury will need to roll $2+ trillion of debt this fiscal year at rates that were unthinkable 18 months ago.

**The market is not pricing:** the municipal bond stress that comes when state and local governments refinance COVID-era debt at current rates. The muni market is quiet now. It won't be for long.
