**Federal Reserve — Warsh in Bangkok**

Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** spoke at the IMF-World Bank Annual Meetings in Bangkok this morning (October 16). Key takeaways:
- No rate signal offered; explicitly rejected forward guidance. "Investors should assess economic data rather than rely on advance policy signals."
- Described the global monetary environment as "synchronous tightening in most places" — allies everyone else's hawkishness with his own.
- On AI: said the technology could drive productivity but the transition would be "**very bumpy**" — that phrase moved headlines.
- Market reaction was mildly positive; Goldman Sachs read the remarks as a **pause signal for October 28**, with Goldman now modeling a final 25 bps hike in December.
- Rate hike probability for October 28 fell post-speech to approximately 15% per some derivatives markets.
- **Communications blackout begins Saturday**. Whatever the market is pricing now is what it'll hold through the October 27-28 meeting.

The Fed raised in September — its first hike since 2023 — to **3.75%-4.00%**. The September hike was reactive to inflation re-accelerating. The open question is whether the September move was enough, or whether Warsh will need to go again before year-end.

## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

**The Week That Was: $1.64 Trillion Evaporated**

Let's not bury the lede. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is down **more than 20% from its June 2026 peak**. The index dropped **3.3% on Thursday alone**. The SMH semiconductor ETF was down **~8% week-to-date** entering Friday. Total market cap destroyed in six sessions across US semiconductor names: approximately **$1.64 trillion**. This is not a garden-variety dip — this is a repricing of the AI capex thesis.

The trigger is CoreWeave (more below in M&A/Corporate), but the underlying cause is the convergence of:
1. **Hot inflation → higher rates → lower DCF valuations for long-duration growth**
2. **AI infrastructure financing stress** — if hyperscaler capex is debt-funded, rising rates raise the cost of the entire AI ecosystem
3. **Chinese competition** — CXMT's progress on advanced memory chips raised incremental competitive concerns for US and Korean memory names

**Nvidia (NVDA)**

The relative out-performer this week, which is itself a story. NVDA fell **1.84% Thursday** but is edging up **+0.27% premarket Friday**. Jensen Huang explicitly said this week that **Nvidia does not need to backstop CoreWeave financially** — that clarification mattered. The question the market is wrestling with: does AI demand remain robust even if the financing structure for AI infrastructure is under stress? Nvidia's order book says yes; its stock is catching a bid while AMD and the memory names get hammered. Watch NVDA's relative strength vs. the SOX — a widening spread between NVDA and the index is the AI trade reconfiguring itself, not dying.

**AMD (AMD)**

Weaker than NVDA. Down **1.5%-4%** depending on the reporting date (multiple sources with varying figures). **TSMC (TSM)**

The canonical "good news is no news" moment: TSMC reported **Q3 net profit up 69%**, a record result. Stock fell **2% Thursday**. **CoreWeave (CRWV)**

The stress test of the AI debt cycle. Premarket down **4% Friday**, reversing Thursday's bounce. The company has approximately **$50 billion in debt and obligations** (vs. $35B disclosed at Q2 end — the number has grown fast). Yields on its 2031 bonds have topped **15%**. Credit default swaps hit distressed levels Thursday per CNBC.

D.A. Davidson's analysis is brutal: CoreWeave borrows at ~9% while generating returns of ~1%. BofA projects $67 billion in cumulative negative free cash flow from 2026-2028 and assumes $59B in additional borrowing will be needed. The company's contracted backlog of $129B (Meta at 27%, OpenAI at 17%) is real. The question is whether that backlog justifies the leverage and whether the financing model survives a sustained higher-rate environment. The answer in the bond market this week has been "not obviously."

If CoreWeave debt stress metastasizes into a broader AI infrastructure credit event, the downstream hit on NVDA, Broadcom, and the hyperscalers themselves would be severe. Right now it looks contained. Right now.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

**What the Tape Is Telling You**

The market spent this week processing a collision between two narratives that had been coexisting: (1) AI is an unstoppable capex cycle that justifies stretched valuations, and (2) higher-for-longer rates are eventually someone's problem. This week, those narratives crashed into each other with $1.64 trillion in semiconductor market cap as the wreckage.

**Theme 1: AI Infrastructure Debt Cycle Is the New Risk Factor**

CoreWeave has become the poster child for a model that nobody examined carefully enough: borrow cheap, buy Nvidia GPUs, lease capacity to hyperscalers, repeat. At 9% borrowing cost vs. 1% return on assets (per D.A. Davidson), this model only works at effectively zero real interest rates. It doesn't work at 5.29% 10-year yields. The market is now asking: if CoreWeave's $50B in debt becomes a problem, who is exposed? The answer is Nvidia (inventory backstop risk), Microsoft (customer), Meta (customer at 27% of backlog), OpenAI (customer at 17%), and indirectly every AI capex name in the portfolio. This is not yet a systemic risk — the backlog is real, the demand is real — but it's the first credible stress scenario for the AI trade.

**Theme 2: Semis Divergence Is Not Random**

NVDA holding up while AMD, Broadcom, Intel, Dell, and the memory names get hammered is a meaningful signal. The market is rewarding "demand" (NVDA, TSMC's revenue growth) and punishing "supply-side AI infrastructure debt" exposure. If you're in the AI trade with a long book spread across the whole chip complex, this week told you to concentrate into the demand names and cut the infrastructure buildout names until the rate picture clears.

**Theme 3: The Rate Hike Re-Acceleration Is Real**

September CPI at 3.7% YoY with a 0.6% monthly print and core at 2.6% is not "disinflation on track." The Fed hiked in September after a two-year pause. Warsh in Bangkok withheld a signal but the market is reading October as a pause with December as the next hike. That means:
- Higher for longer is the base case through at least Q1 2027
- Tech multiple compression is not over
- Every leveraged balance sheet in the AI ecosystem needs to be stress-tested

**Theme 4: The Yen Is the Forgotten Risk**

USD/JPY at 158, flirting with 160 where BoJ intervention becomes probable, is a tail risk that nobody is talking about loudly enough. A disorderly yen intervention episode — like August 2024 — would be a carry trade unwind that hits global equities broadly and tech specifically. The correlation between USD/JPY carries and tech positioning is structurally significant.

**What the Market Is Not Pricing That It Should Be**

1. **The second-order effects of CoreWeave stress.** If CoreWeave needs to slow its GPU purchases or restructure, Nvidia's near-term backlog is more at risk than the stock is pricing. The market is treating this as a CoreWeave-specific problem.
2. **October 28 FOMC is live.** Goldman's base case is a pause, but if there's any upside surprise in the retail sales data or a second hot inflation print before then, the odds flip. A surprise hike into this tech selloff would be brutal.
3. **China memory competition is structurally underweighted.** CXMT's progress on advanced memory gets flagged one day and forgotten the next. If Chinese producers crack the advanced memory market at scale, the impairment to Micron, SK Hynix, and Samsung is not a quarter or two — it's structural margin degradation.
