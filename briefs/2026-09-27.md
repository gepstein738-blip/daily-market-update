## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve — The September Hike Is the Story of the Week
The FOMC voted 12-0 on September 16 to raise the federal funds rate by 25bp to a range of **3.75%-4.00%** — the first rate hike since 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh statement: "Inflation remains elevated... today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal." He went further in the press conference, describing inflation as "too high...for too long." 

The September dot plot:
- Core PCE 2026 forecast: **3.4%** (revised up 0.1%)
- Core PCE 2027: 2.5%
- Core PCE 2028: 2.1%
- Median dots: Fed funds unchanged at 4.1% in 2027, then cuts to 3.9% in 2028 and 3.6% in 2029

**Read this correctly:** The Fed is NOT signaling one-and-done. The 2027 median funds rate at 4.1% means there's effectively another 10bp hike baked in — or, more likely, a prolonged plateau that takes rates through at least 2026-Q4 before any discussion of easing. The bond market printed 5.17% on the 10Y in response. That's the market's verdict.

**Political dimension:** President Trump has publicly and repeatedly called for rate cuts and has targeted Warsh directly. This tension is non-trivial — it introduces uncertainty about the Fed's operational independence if political pressure intensifies into 2027, an election year context. Markets are pricing Warsh as holding firm, but any signal of capitulation would crater the dollar and spike the long end.

### Labor Market
August NFP: **+162,000** jobs — strongest monthly print since March 2026 and well ahead of expectations. Prior months had been "relatively feeble" per descriptions. This is the data that cemented the September hike. A strong labor market + elevated inflation = no reason to pause. Watch September NFP (due first Friday of October) as the next critical input.

### International Central Banks
- **ECB:** Market pricing in one more 25bp hike before year-end, but Eurozone data is weakening. French and German PMIs have been soft. ECB's optionality is narrowing.
- **BOJ:** Stuck. USD/JPY at 157 reflects the paralysis. BOJ hiking aggressively would shock JGB markets; not hiking means continued yen erosion. No resolution in sight.
- **PBOC:** Stimulative bias. Chinese property sector -19.9% YTD investment is a crisis in slow motion. Industrial production holding up, but downstream demand — and therefore global commodity demand — looks vulnerable.

### Geopolitical: US-Iran War / Energy Crisis
The **2026 Iran War Fuel Crisis** is not a background risk — it's an active supply shock. Key facts:
- US-Iran strikes intensified in early September, disrupting Strait of Hormuz (20%+ of world oil trade)
- September 11: East-West Crude Oil Pipeline shut down, disrupting 39% of global trade flows
- Brent reached ~$110 at peak; has pulled back to $104 on thin diplomatic signals
- Iran announcing a "new Hormuz shipping route" — market skepticism warranted
- The Philippines declared an energy crisis related to the disruption

**This is a tail risk that has been partially priced but not fully absorbed.** A full Strait of Hormuz closure at current oil inventory levels would be a genuine macro shock.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

**What the tape is saying:**

The week ending September 25 was a statement: the market can rally even with a rate hike, 5.17% 10Y yields, and an active Middle East conflict, as long as AI momentum is intact. Monday's surge (S&P +1.49%, Nasdaq +2.26% to a record close) came from three pillars simultaneously improving — yields retreating briefly, oil pulling back on Hormuz diplomacy, and AI sentiment roaring back via AMD's $1T milestone and Meta's Muse viral moment. That's a fragile tripod, and Friday's S&P close at 7,743 — lower than Monday's 7,765 — shows the tripod is wobbling.

**Dominant themes this week:**

1. **AI product vs. AI infrastructure bifurcation.** The market is rewarding companies with tangible AI product momentum (META, AMD) more than infrastructure pure-plays (NVDA is flat while AMD adds $100B in market cap). The Muse phenomenon specifically is shifting the narrative: AI isn't just capex and GPU orders — it's a consumer product race, and Meta just fired a shot at OpenAI/Microsoft's consumer moat.

2. **Rate regime reset.** The Fed's September hike is a regime change, not a one-off. Warsh is not Yellen or Powell — he is institutionally hawkish, and the 2027 dot-plot at 4.1% makes this a "higher for longer" story well into 2027. Models that assumed a 2026 cutting cycle are broken; re-discount accordingly.

3. **Oil as the joker card.** $104 Brent is already baked into CPI math for Q4 2026. If Hormuz escalates further, you're looking at $120+ Brent and a genuine inflation re-acceleration that forces the Fed's hand harder. This is the risk nobody is pricing for because everyone assumes diplomacy resolves it.

4. **Yield curve normalization.** The 2Y/10Y spread has moved from deeply inverted to +36bp positive. Historically, curve un-inversion precedes recessions — the signal isn't "good economy" but rather "the market is pricing rate cuts *eventually*" even as Warsh signals no cuts until 2028. This curve dynamic is worth monitoring weekly.

**What the market is NOT pricing that it should:**

- **Full Hormuz closure.** The market is trading oil on diplomatic optimism. The East-West pipeline shutdown and the underlying military situation suggest a far worse scenario is more likely than priced. Any escalation from current levels means $120+ Brent and a genuine Fed dilemma: fight inflation from energy or protect growth.
- **Muse inference costs.** META's 36% September run prices in the product success but not the compute bill. At 3.4M users and growing, the inference costs on a personal AI agent are not trivial. This is a 2027 margin question that will arrive faster than the street thinks.
- **AMD-INTC competitive dynamics.** AMD just crossed $1T and Intel is up 200% YTD — the market is pricing BOTH winning in AI. That's probably not how the custom ASIC vs. GPU vs. CPU story resolves; one of these multiples is wrong.
- **OpenAI IPO 2027 arrival.** When OpenAI does finally go public, it will reprice the entire AI landscape. The delay is good for incumbents (MSFT, GOOG, META) for now, but the eventual IPO creates a new competitor for AI mindshare and capital allocation.
- **China's property collapse downstream effects.** Chinese fixed asset investment -7.2% YTD, property -19.9%. Copper at $6.70 is already pricing some of this. But if Chinese demand softens materially in Q4, you lose a pillar of global commodities demand at exactly the moment when oil supply is being squeezed. Stagflationary.
