## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve
The Fed executed its **first rate hike in three-plus years** at the September meeting, taking the funds rate to **4.00%** in a 12-0 unanimous vote. The move had been well-telegraphed but the vote count (unanimity is notable) signaled institutional hawkish resolve. What changed overnight: NY Fed President John Williams, the committee's second-ranking voice, explicitly **pushed back on an October move**, stating there is "no need for urgency." Markets have consequently marked down October probability from ~70% earlier this week to **below 50%** now. The Fed's two remaining meetings are October 27-28 and December 9.

The key read: August Core PCE came in at **3.0%**, materially below the ~3.4% headline CPI — this is what gave Williams political cover to signal a pause. But at 3.0%, core PCE is still 100bps above target, and the September Jobs Report (tomorrow, Friday Oct 2) will set the tone for October's debate. Consensus is **100,000 jobs** and **4.2% unemployment**. That's a relatively benign print — if it misses significantly to the upside, October is back on the table.

Fed Governor Lisa Cook flagged that **AI is adding inflationary pressure** to the economy via energy demand and hardware capacity constraints. This is an underappreciated structural point: the AI capex supercycle isn't just a growth story, it's a cost story flowing through to prices.

## 4. TECH SECTOR DEEP DIVE

### Alphabet — GOOGL: The Biggest Story Today

Google launched **Gemini 4 Argon** on October 1, positioning it as a direct assault on OpenAI's o3-level reasoning models and Anthropic's Claude 4-series. This is not an incremental update.

**Key benchmarks:**
- **77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1** (long-horizon software engineering)
- **91.7% on LVBench** (long video understanding)
- **51.3% on AutomationBench** (ranked #1)
- Output limit expanded from 64K to **1 million tokens**
- Initial deployment: cybersecurity partners first, then paid API and Google AI Ultra subscribers

**Pricing:** $2/M input tokens, $10/M output tokens — **95% discount on cached input**. This pricing undercuts prior frontier model pricing aggressively. The implication for the Microsoft/OpenAI axis is real: if enterprise customers can get Gemini 4 Argon quality at these prices, Azure OpenAI service needs to respond. Watch for MSFT/OpenAI pricing action.

**The strategic read:** Google is deploying Argon in cybersecurity first — this is not accidental. Cybersecurity is a beachhead for agentic AI (automated threat response, autonomous remediation) where token count matters enormously. The 1M output window is a structural moat for multi-step enterprise workflows.

GOOGL stock is +2% on the news — a measured reaction that suggests the market sees this as expected execution, not a surprise. But watch for enterprise software names (ServiceNow, Salesforce, Palo Alto) to see pricing pressure read-throughs.

### Micron — MU: Blowout Print, Sell-the-News

Micron reported the most startling quarterly numbers in its history and the stock is -1% in the premarket. Classic sell-the-news — but don't let the action confuse you about the substance.

**Q4 FY2026:**
- Revenue: **$54.23B** vs. $50.45B estimate — **+379% year-over-year**
- EPS: **$33.42** vs. $31.16 estimate
- Gross margin: **87%** (+210bps sequentially)
- Operating margin: **82.3%**
- Full-year FY2026 revenue: $133.2B, up **256% YoY**
- Full-year diluted EPS: $75.52, up **811% YoY**

**Q1 FY2027 Guidance:**
- Revenue midpoint: **$61.5B** (31% sequential growth)
- Adjusted EPS: **$38.15**

The numbers look wrong at first glance — 379% revenue growth, 87% gross margin on a memory chip company — but they're real. HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) for AI accelerators has become a pricing oligopoly, and Micron is one of three players globally (alongside SK Hynix and Samsung). NVDA can't ship Blackwell chips without HBM, and lead times are measured in months. The -1% stock reaction is position unwind, not a fundamental rejection.

**Read-through for NVDA, AMAT, LRCX:** Micron's blowout confirms AI accelerator demand shows no sign of slowing. Memory infrastructure is a rate-of-change proxy for the whole AI buildout.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape is Telling You

The market is doing something unusual right now: it is sustaining AI earnings-driven multiple expansion while simultaneously repricing the long end of the curve higher at a pace that should be killing equity valuations. This contradiction is being resolved by a bifurcation: anything with a credible AI earnings story (NVDA, MU, ACN, GOOGL, MSFT) is holding or grinding higher; everything that depends on rate sensitivity (utilities ex-nuclear, REITs, rate-sensitive consumer, long-duration bonds) is getting hit.

**The 10-year at 5.30% is the single most important number in markets right now.** Every equity desk has a model where this starts to matter. Most of them have it in the 5.25–5.50% range. We're testing that upper bound. If the September NFP (Friday) prints meaningfully above 100K, yields could spike toward 5.5%, and the equity bid could evaporate rapidly.

**Accenture's +17% is the most underappreciated signal.** Professional services firms don't get 17% single-day moves on ordinary beats. The market is repricing the "AI implementation revenues" category in real time. The firms best positioned to capture enterprise AI transformation (ACN, IBM, Cognizant, Infosys) may be in a multi-quarter earnings revision cycle that hasn't fully been priced.

**Micron's numbers don't look real — and they are.** Q4 revenue of $54B for a memory chip company at an 87% gross margin is what happens when HBM supply for AI accelerators is structurally constrained and you're one of three global manufacturers. The read-through for the entire AI infrastructure ecosystem (wafer equipment, packaging, advanced substrate makers) is unambiguously positive.

**Gemini 4 Argon is a competitive escalation, not just a product launch.** Google is back in the frontier model race. Argon's 1M output limit and $2/M token pricing for input is aggressive pricing strategy designed to build API adoption before competitors can respond. The AI model cost curve is still deflating — this is negative for anyone whose business model depends on model pricing staying high (some startups, boutique AI API providers), but positive for enterprise adoption broadly.

**Risks to the current consensus:**

1. **Treasury yields are the elephant in the room.** The consensus is that equities can absorb 5.30% because AI earnings justify the multiple. That thesis fails somewhere between 5.50% and 5.75% on the 10Y — not because of some theoretical model, but because that's where pension funds start forced selling and risk parity funds get redemptions.

2. **China reopens October 8 into a week of no data.** Golden Week silence means no economic signals. When mainland investors return, they'll be reacting to: a Fed that may not hike in October, oil slightly lower, dollar stronger, and global AI stocks higher. The net reaction is uncertain — there's risk in both directions, particularly in commodities and dual-listed tech names.

3. **The Anthropic IPO is the knife in the market's pocket.** If Anthropic prices at $2 trillion in October, it will suck oxygen from every other growth allocation. Funds that want Anthropic exposure will sell existing AI holdings to fund the IPO allocation. Expect NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL to face technical headwinds around the pricing date.

4. **Nike's gross margin contraction is a leading indicator for consumer-facing tariff pass-through.** A 320bps gross margin decline despite a revenue beat tells you tariffs are landing in corporate P&Ls, not just in trade statistics. The consumer sector hasn't fully repriced this.
