## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### Federal Reserve — The Regime Change Is Real

The week's dominant macro event: FOMC minutes released Wednesday. The Fed raised its target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%** at the September meeting — its first hike since 2023, voted 12-0. Chairman Kevin Warsh framed it unambiguously: inflation remains elevated, the 2% target is not yet in sight. The minutes themselves showed near-unanimous agreement that another 25bp hike is likely before year-end.

What changed: this is no longer a "higher for longer" environment. It is a **re-tightening** environment. The market had been pricing a Q2 2027 pivot; that timeline is being pushed out with every FOMC statement. Fed funds futures now price ~4.1% by January and ~4.7% by October 2027. The equity market's P/E expansion over the past two years was predicated on anticipated rate relief. That relief has reversed.

**Next FOMC:** October 27–28. October 14 CPI print will be the deciding input. If headline CPI comes in ≥0.5% MoM (plausible with Brent's 4.8% surge today), a November hike becomes a live probability.

### September Jobs Report (+29K) — The Warning Nobody Is Heeding

Released October 2 but still the primary labor data. September payrolls: **+29,000** — a massive miss versus all surveyed estimates. Unemployment: **4.2%** (trending higher from 4.1%). Sectors contracting: information, professional/business services, financial activities, government (-17K). Healthcare was the lone positive contributor.

This is the data that should dominate the macro narrative and doesn't, because energy/geopolitical noise is crowding it out. +29K jobs in a month is the kind of number that, without the war premium in oil and a Fed forced to re-tighten on inflation, would be a recession-watch signal. The disconnect between this data and current equity valuations is a vulnerability. Watch October payrolls (release date: first Friday of November) as the confirming or refuting data point.

**Wage growth:** Slowing. Some comfort on inflation persistence, but little use to an economy where job creation has essentially stalled.

### India: RBI MPC Update

The Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee raised its FY2027 CPI inflation forecast to **5.2%** and projected GDP growth at 7.1%. India CPI rose to 4.8% in August from 4.5% in July. Separately, India noted that 37% of its CPI basket is now rising above 4% — a breadth signal that inflation is not concentrated but structural. RBI hawkish signals are a headwind for Indian equity valuations, which have been a crowded EM long.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

### What the Tape Is Telling You

The market is staring at a three-factor macro shock that historically does not resolve cleanly: (1) an energy inflation re-acceleration driven by geopolitical supply risk (Brent +4.8% today, +27% from conflict onset); (2) a labor market in visible deterioration (+29K September payrolls); and (3) a central bank that cannot cut because inflation remains above target, and has just re-started hiking. This is not the soft-landing scenario. It is the stagflation-adjacent scenario the last two years of Fed communication tried to prevent.

The equity market is not pricing this fully. Here is why it has been slow to react: AI infrastructure capex is providing genuine earnings support for the top 10 names; corporate balance sheets are still healthy from the 2021–2022 cash-build cycle; and buyback flows are mechanically absorbing selling pressure. These buffers are real but finite.

### The Yield-Multiple Reckoning

The 10-year at 5.36% (highest since 2002) is the single most important number in the market. At that level: (a) the equity risk premium on the S&P 500 at current multiples is approximately zero; (b) the DCF discount rate for high-multiple growth stocks becomes punishing; (c) consumer credit costs (mortgage, auto, card) are at levels that are starting to visibly reduce demand. The late arrival of rate-sensitivity into the equity complex — compared to 2022's faster repricing — reflects the AI-capex earnings shield. That shield is not permanent.

### The AI Differentiation Trade

The AI trade is maturing. H1 2026 was "buy everything AI-adjacent." Q3 2026 is the beginning of "prove the economics." The signals:
- APLD's 155% revenue beat: AI data center demand is real, economics are visible, this is a winner
- PLTR's Goldman upgrade: Defense AI is a funded, contracted, recurring revenue story
- MRVL's ASIC wins: Custom silicon for hyperscalers is structurally taking share from commodity accelerators
- SPCX raising $40B in debt to buy NVDA chips: Market punished this — return on GPU capital is now being questioned, not assumed

The stocks that can demonstrate clear AI revenue generation with improving margins will hold. The stocks that are spending aggressively with unclear payback timelines will face multiple compression regardless of AI narrative. This is the stock-picker's market within the AI theme.

### The Consumer Time Bomb

This is being slept on. The aggregate:
- LEVI: DTC miss despite headline beat
- PEP: trimmed outlook despite Q3 beat
- LULU: -20% on disappointing earnings and guidance (recent)
- Best Buy: stock fell despite a quarterly beat and raised outlook
- September payrolls: +29K

The consumer is value-trading down, delaying discretionary purchases, and absorbing higher food/energy prices with reduced budget for everything else. This pattern, ahead of the holiday season, with mortgage rates at multi-year highs and student loan payments back in effect, is a serious risk to Q4 consumer discretionary earnings. The retail sector is not priced for this.

### What the Market Is Not Pricing That It Should Be

**1. An October 14 CPI surprise:** Brent gained 4.8% today. The September CPI basket closes on September 30, so today's move is not in the September number — but the base level was already elevated. If September CPI prints 0.5%+ MoM, a second consecutive hawkish data print forces the October 28 FOMC into live hike territory. Market is pricing ~40% probability of an October hike; that should be closer to 60% given the current trajectory.

**2. Strait of Hormuz tail risk:** A genuine supply disruption would remove 18–20M b/d of throughput. The G7 reserve release (100M barrels) covers approximately 5–6 days of Strait throughput. Brent above $110 sustained changes the CPI math categorically and would likely force an emergency Fed response. This is not priced at any meaningful probability.

**3. Labor market hard landing:** +29K September jobs against a backdrop of 3.75–4.00% fed funds is structurally contractionary. If October delivers a similar number, "soft landing" is no longer the base case — it is an aspirational scenario. The market consensus embed for a 2027 soft-landing-led recovery will need to be revisited.

**4. Consensus complacency:** The dominant trade is: AI is a secular bull, the Fed cuts in 2027, energy is transitory. If any two of these assumptions break simultaneously (Iran disruption + Fed hike + consumer miss), the repricing is disorderly. Positioning for this tail is cheap right now; the options market is not fully reflecting the correlation risk.
