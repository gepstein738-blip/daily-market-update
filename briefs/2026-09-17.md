## 3. MACRO AND POLICY

### The Fed — The Central Event
The FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate 25bp to 3.75%–4.00% on Wednesday, September 16 — the first hike since July 2023. This is not a surprise in isolation, but the *hawkishness of the message* is what moved markets.

Chair Kevin Warsh said inflation has been "too high for too long" and that the Fed needed to be "confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective clearly and at sufficient speed." The dot plot is the tell: 16 of 18 members project at least one more hike this year. The implied terminal rate is now 4.25%–4.50% or higher, with markets now pricing 3+ additional hikes through mid-2027.

The bigger issue for equity investors: the Fed is explicitly moving away from the "wait and see" posture it maintained through much of 2024-2025. Warsh wants front-loaded tightening to avoid a 1970s repeat. If inflation data doesn't cooperate in the next 3-4 months, the Fed will hike again at the November or December meeting.

**What the market is not fully pricing:** The interaction between 5%+ 10-year yields and equity multiples at 27-28x forward earnings on the S&P 500. At current rates, the "risk premium" for equities has essentially evaporated. This is the structural tension, not a tactical worry.

### Bank of England
Held at 3.75% this morning in a 6-3 vote. The three dissenters wanted a hike. The statement language on inflation was dovish on its surface but the dissent structure tells you the MPC is not done. Next meeting: November.

### Bank of Japan (Imminent)
Meeting concludes September 18. All 52 polled economists expect a 25bp hike from current levels. BOJ Governor Ueda has explicitly signaled readiness, citing "upside price risks." 93% of surveyed economists see another hike by January. This is the most consequential central bank action for global bond markets after the Fed — yen carry trade unwind risk is real if the BOJ hikes *and* signals accelerated normalization.

### New Zealand GDP
Q2 2026 GDP: +0.2% QoQ (above 0.1% forecast), +2.6% YoY. Weakest quarterly pace since Q2 2025. RBNZ has less room to cut than the market assumed. Minor data point but consistent with the global "growth OK, inflation sticky" narrative.

### Geopolitics / Commodities
The Saudi Arabia East-West pipeline disruption (shut, removing alternative crude export route avoiding the Strait of Hormuz) was the key geopolitical oil driver this week. Early signals Thursday suggest the disruption may resolve faster than feared. Watch this closely — if the pipeline stays shut and any Hormuz tension emerges, oil moves hard and fast. That's a secondary inflation shock the Fed does not want.

## 8. MARKET THEMES AND NARRATIVE

**Theme 1: The Fed is Back, and the Market Hasn't Fully Digested It**
The FOMC's September 16 hike is not the problem — the problem is the dot plot. Sixteen of 18 officials want at least one more move this year. The market spent much of 2024-2025 convinced the Fed's next move was a cut. That consensus has now been inverted. The repricing from "Fed on hold" to "Fed hiking again" takes months to work through equity multiples, especially in long-duration tech. The Thursday bounce is relief, not resolution.

**Theme 2: AI Power Infrastructure Has Officially Become a Sector**
The Generac-Amazon deal is the clearest signal yet that AI infrastructure is consuming massive real-world resources, and the supply chain for those resources (backup power, liquid cooling, copper, transformers) is being locked up at the corporate level via long-term contracts and equity structures. This is the "picks and shovels" trade one layer deeper — not the GPU makers, but the people who keep the GPUs running. Eaton (ETN), GE Vernova (GEV), Vertiv (VRT), Generac (GNRC), and Bloom Energy (BE) are the names. The Blackstone acquisition of Flow Control confirms institutional capital is arriving.

**Theme 3: AI Monetization Is No Longer Theoretical**
Meta One at $7.99/month across 3.5B users is the clearest path to real AI revenue we've seen from Big Tech. Snap's Specs Intelligence is a credible consumer AI device. OpenAI at $1.2T valuation means the private market believes AI revenue is enormous. The question now is *which public companies capture the most monetization.* This is no longer a capex-only story.

**Theme 4: Cybersecurity Crowding**
Bernstein's triple downgrade of PANW/OKTA/S is a warning shot at the "AI + security" thematic trade that has doubled in 2026. The fundamentals are fine. The valuation and positioning are not. When the best-in-class firm that has covered these names longest says "fairly valued," take it seriously. Trim the winners.

**Theme 5: What the Market Is Not Pricing**
(a) The interaction between 5%+ 10-year yields and 27-28x forward S&P multiples. This is the slow-moving train wreck. At 5% risk-free, you are paying for equity risk premium that isn't there on a relative value basis.  
(b) The BOJ hike tomorrow. If Ueda hikes AND signals more to come, the yen carry trade ($4+ trillion in estimated funded carry positions) starts to unwind. We got a preview of this in August 2024 when the Nikkei dropped 12% in a day. Scale of the carry book is larger now.  
(c) The oil risk from the Saudi pipeline disruption has not gone away — it has just been forgotten for 48 hours while the Fed dominated. If that disruption worsens, you have oil at $115+ and the Fed faces a simultaneous supply-side inflation shock. That is not in any current consensus model.
